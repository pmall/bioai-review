# Structure Prediction Models: Lineages, Critics, and the Road to Design

**What this document is for.** This is the predictor-side foundation for a review of AI binder generation. Every generative pipeline reviewed later in this project stands on a structure predictor in one of three ways, and this doc establishes, per lineage, what that predictor is and how far it can be trusted. It is *not* a general survey of structure prediction: sections are chosen and weighted by what the design reports will need to cite.

**Scope note.** These are **structure prediction** models: every chemical entity's identity (protein sequence, DNA/RNA sequence, ligand identity) is user-specified input; the model's only job is to infer *geometry*. Contrast with RFdiffusion (invents geometry) and ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN (invents sequence), each of which already has its own report in `literature/reports/`.

**Confidence flag, read this first.** All twelve primary sources below are paper-read: **AlphaFold2, AlphaFold3, Boltz-1, Boltz-2, Protenix-v1, Protenix-v2, OpenDDE, Chai-1, RoseTTAFold, RFAA, ESM2/ESMFold1, ESMC/ESMFold2** — every one present in `literature/corpus/` and read directly. **OpenFold3-preview** is the exception and stays at repo-read level: no paper, no DOI, map-only by design (`literature/map.md`). It is now partly rescued by third-party benchmarking — Protenix-v2 and OpenDDE both run OpenFold3-preview2 as a baseline (§2, §5), so we have numbers for it even without its own paper. One source sits *outside* this corpus: the earlier **Protenix AF3-reproduction technical report (2024/2025)**, which an earlier draft conflated with the cataloged `protenix_v1` entry; §4 carries the correction and the list of withdrawn claims. Errors already caught and fixed during this project — a misremembered ESMFold2 MSA capability, an assumed Boltz no-MSA mode, OpenFold3 treated as published, the Protenix version mix-up, and an inverted Boltz-1 physical-validity rate — are why this flag exists.

**Deliberately excluded.** Design-side descendants get their own reports: RFdiffusion 1/2/3 and ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN (already written), BindCraft, AlphaProteo, BoltzGen, PXDesign, Chai-2, Latent-X, and the flow-matching family. Where a paper bundles prediction and design (Protenix-v2, ESMC/ESMFold2), only the prediction half is treated here and the design half is flagged for the later pass. Also out: the ESM Atlas and ESMC world-model atlas (databases, not models), mechanistic-interpretability findings beyond the MSA question, ESM3 (scoped out project-wide), Chai-3 and IsoDDE (commercial, no publication — though IsoDDE surfaces in §5 as a benchmark reference level, which is the best handle on it we have).

## 1. Framing: what a predictor is *for* in a binder-design pipeline

### 1.1 Three ways a predictor couples to a generator

The distinction that matters most for the later reports is **how tightly** the predictor is bound to the generator. Three regimes, in increasing order of coupling:

- **Level 0 — inherited weights.** The generator *is* a repurposed predictor. RFdiffusion (`10.1038/s41586-023-06415-8`) is a fine-tuned RoseTTAFold; RFdiffusion3 (`10.1101/2025.09.18.676967`), BoltzGen (`10.1101/2025.11.20.689494`), Chai-2 (`10.1101/2025.07.05.663018`) and Protenix-v2's design mode (`10.64898/2026.04.10.717613`) all descend from the predictors in this doc. Here the predictor's inductive biases are baked into the generator and cannot be swapped out.
- **Level 1 — post-hoc critic.** Generate first, then predict the complex and filter on the predictor's own confidence. This is the field's default success criterion: AlphaProteo (`10.48550/arXiv.2409.08022`) defines success as *"interchain AF2 pAE < 10, binder-aligned binder RMSD < 1 Å, pLDDT > 80"*; BoltzGen (`10.1101/2025.11.20.689494`) filters its 10,000-design pools by refolding with Boltz-2 and keeping RMSD < 2.5 Å. The predictor never sees a gradient.
- **Level 2 — differentiable objective.** The predictor's loss is backpropagated into the sequence being designed. **BindCraft** (`10.1038/s41586-025-09429-6`) does this through AF2 multimer, producing an *L*×20 error gradient over amino-acid choices per position, annealed through four stages from continuous logits to one-hot. **ESMC/ESMFold2** does it through both the folding head *and the 6B-parameter language model*: *"Our approach represents sequences as continuous distributions over amino acid probabilities and optimizes by backpropagation through ESMC and ESMFold2"* — and reports that *"a single-stage optimization over continuous sequences is sufficient"*, against BindCraft's four. **PXDesign-h** (`10.1101/2025.08.15.670450`) is Protenix's entry in the same class.

Level 2 is the current frontier, and it imposes a hard requirement that turns out to be this doc's central thesis in disguise.

### 1.2 Why MSA-emancipation is a precondition for Level 2, not just an accuracy story

To backpropagate into a sequence, every step from sequence to predicted structure must be differentiable. **An MSA is a database lookup on a sequence that does not exist yet** — it is neither differentiable nor even defined for a binder being invented. So a Level-2 pipeline must run its predictor on the designed chain in single-sequence mode, whatever that predictor's preferred regime.

This is why BindCraft works but is fighting its tool: AF2 is documented in its own paper (§2) to degrade sharply below ~30 MSA sequences, so hallucinating through it means operating the model in precisely its weakest configuration, and BindCraft's elaborate multi-stage annealing plus five-model ensembling reads as compensation for exactly that. ESMC/ESMFold2 has no such tension — single-sequence *is* its trained regime, and gradients flow through a learned 6B prior rather than stopping at a feature layer.

So the MSA axis this doc tracks is not merely about accuracy or convenience. **It sets a ceiling on how tightly a lineage's predictor can be coupled to a generator.** Read every section below with that question attached: what does this model do when handed a sequence with no evolutionary history?

### 1.4 The MSA taxonomy

- **Keep it, and keep building on it:** RoseTTAFold/RFAA, OpenFold3-preview, Boltz-1, OpenDDE, and — pointedly — Protenix-v1, whose 2026 release *adds* RNA MSAs and protein templates, moving away from emancipation rather than toward it (§4).
- **Keep it, but demote it inside the architecture:** AlphaFold3. MSA processing shrinks to four blocks and the MSA representation is discarded after them, so "MSA-based" means "MSA-seeded pair representation," not AF2's coevolution-centric trunk (§2).
- **Keep it as one option among several:** Chai-1 — a full MSA track and a protein-LM track side by side, either usable alone (§6).
- **Replace it with a single sequence through a large pretrained LM:** ESM2/ESMFold1, then ESMC/ESMFold2, which retains a detachable MSA encoder purely as a rescue path for sequences the LM scores as high-perplexity (§7).
- **Add what MSA never provided (affinity, dynamics) on top of an MSA-based backbone:** Boltz-2 (§3).

### 1.5 One piece of good luck for comparability

**AF3, Boltz-1, Protenix-v1 and Protenix-v2 all train to a 2021-09-30 PDB cutoff.** Boltz-1 and Protenix also both build MSAs with the ColabFold/MMseqs2 pipeline. Where this doc compares those four, the comparison is unusually clean. Where it reaches outside that set — Chai-1 (2021-01-12 cutoff), ESMFold2, OpenDDE, Boltz-2 (2023-06-01, and see §8 on leakage) — it is not. §8 collects the caveats.

## 2. AlphaFold2 → AlphaFold3, and the open reproductions

**AlphaFold2 (Jumper et al., *Nature* 2021, `10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2`).** First method to reach near-experimental single-chain accuracy broadly (median 0.96 Å Cα r.m.s.d.₉₅ on CASP14 vs. 2.8 Å for the next-best method — roughly the width of two carbon atoms of difference). Two-stage architecture: a **trunk** of 48 **Evoformer** blocks jointly refines an *N*seq×*N*res MSA representation and an *N*res×*N*res pair representation, followed by an 8-block (shared-weight) **structure module**.

- **Evoformer's core idea:** treat structure prediction as graph inference in 3D, with pair-representation entries as directed graph edges. Two triangle-based update mechanisms enforce that the pairwise distances stay consistent with basic geometric constraints (the triangle inequality): **triangle multiplicative updates** (a cheap, non-attention way to update one edge of a triangle from the other two) and **triangle self-attention** around the starting/ending node. The paper found the combination of both is more accurate than either alone — a rare case of a clean, reported ablation showing two mechanisms are individually sufficient but jointly better. The **MSA→pair outer-product-mean update runs every block**, not once, keeping the two representations in continuous communication — explicitly contrasted with prior work that only did this once.
- **Structure module / IPA:** represents each residue as an independent rigid-body frame (a "residue gas" — free-floating rotation+translation per residue, all initialized at the origin). **Invariant Point Attention (IPA)** augments ordinary attention with 3D query/key/value points computed in each residue's local frame, then projected into the global frame for interaction and back — guaranteeing the whole operation is invariant to global rotation/translation by construction (proven directly in the paper, not just assumed). This is the single biggest architectural fork-point in the whole lineage: AF2 buys correctness-by-construction at the cost of only working naturally on residue-level polymer frames — a real constraint AF3 later abandons entirely (see below).
- **A genuinely surprising design choice:** peptide bond geometry is **deliberately left unconstrained** during the structure module's iterative refinement — the network is *observed* to violate the chain constraint routinely, because breaking it lets every part of the structure refine locally without solving hard loop-closure problems. Exact bond geometry is only restored by a post-hoc Amber force-field relaxation — which the paper states **does not improve GDT/lDDT accuracy**, only removes distracting stereochemical violations. This is a first, mild version of the "accuracy and physical plausibility are separable axes" finding that Boltz-1/1x later turns into a whole inference-time correction mechanism.
- **Loss:** Frame Aligned Point Error (FAPE) — compares predicted to true atom positions under every possible per-residue-frame alignment, clamped L1. Stated as the main source of chirality correctness in the whole model (no separate chirality loss).
- **Training tricks with real measured impact:** **recycling** (running the whole network repeatedly, feeding outputs back as inputs, 3x) meaningfully improves accuracy for minor extra training cost; **self-distillation** (predict structures for ~350K unlabeled Uniclust sequences, filter to high-confidence, retrain on a PDB+distillation mix) measurably improves accuracy — an early example of a technique (using the model's own high-confidence outputs as training data) that recurs later in Boltz-2's distillation strategy; a **BERT-style masked-MSA objective**, trained jointly with the structure loss, encourages the network to learn coevolutionary relationships without hand-coding a specific correlation statistic into the input features.
- **Self-disclosed limitations (both confirmed directly in-paper):** accuracy drops substantially below ~30 median MSA sequences, with gains largely plateauing past ~100 — the earliest and cleanest statement of the MSA-depth threshold effect this whole doc's thesis rests on. And AF2 is **measurably weaker on proteins with few intra-chain/homotypic contacts relative to heterotypic contacts** — i.e., bridging domains whose shape is created almost entirely by contacts with *other* chains in a complex — an early crack in single-chain-only prediction that AF2's own discussion explicitly predicts a future multi-complex system will need to fix. That system is what AF3 became.
- **Confidence calibration:** pLDDT correlates with true lDDT-Cα at Pearson r=0.76; pTM correlates with true TM-score at r=0.85 — both reported as well-calibrated on a large uncurated recent-PDB sample, not just the CASP14 benchmark set.

**AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al., *Nature* 2024, `10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w`).** Generalizes AF2 to arbitrary biomolecular complexes — proteins, DNA, RNA, ligands, ions, covalent modifications — replacing two of AF2's central mechanisms rather than extending them:

- **Evoformer → Pairformer, MSA processing sharply de-emphasized.** MSA embedding shrinks to just 4 blocks using inexpensive pair-weighted averaging; critically, **the MSA representation is not retained after that point** — only the resulting pair representation carries information into the 48-block Pairformer trunk. This is a real, measured step down in how much AF3 leans on the Evoformer's original "coevolution as primary signal" bet, well before Chai-1 or ESMC push the idea further. The paper's own discussion states this explicitly: the lack of cross-entity evolutionary information is "not a substantial blocker to progress" on interaction prediction — a direct textual anticipation of the MSA-emancipation thesis this doc opened with.
- **Structure module (IPA, equivariant) → diffusion module (non-equivariant), a deliberate trade.** AF3's diffusion module denoises raw atom coordinates directly, with **no rotational/translational invariance or equivariance built into the architecture at all** — a stated, deliberate simplification, justified by AF2 ablations showing that removing structure-module complexity had only a modest accuracy cost. Robustness to rotation/translation is instead *learned*, via randomly rotating/translating inputs during training and inference. This is the single change with the largest downstream consequences in this whole doc: Boltz-1 later identifies and fixes a genuine failure mode in how this randomized-rotation training interacts with reverse-diffusion interpolation (the Kabsch-alignment fix), and RFD3 inherits the same no-equivariance diffusion approach wholesale.
- **Stereochemical violation losses eliminated, not just reduced** — AF2 needed carefully tuned violation penalties to enforce chemical plausibility; AF3's multiscale diffusion (low noise levels sharpen local stereochemistry, high noise levels handle global structure) makes this largely unnecessary architecturally, which is precisely what lets AF3 handle arbitrary ligand chemistry without hand-coded bonding-pattern logic. This is also, candidly, the direct root cause of the physical-validity problem Boltz-steering was later built to fix: **AF3's own paper self-reports a 4.4% chirality violation rate** on PoseBusters despite receiving correct-chirality reference features, plus a persistent tendency toward clashing/overlapping atoms, worst in homomers. On cross-paper comparison, be careful here: AF3's own detailed physical-validity panel (Extended Data Fig. 4f) is run on PoseBusters **V2** (crystal contacts removed), not the V1 set used for the headline ligand comparison, and its PB-valid rate is readable only off the figure, so no exact number can be quoted from the paper's text. What *is* directly quotable is Boltz-1's independent measurement: ~58% of AF3's top-1 poses pass all physical checks on Boltz-1's own held-out test set (§3). Treat the apparent agreement between those two as suggestive rather than as a confirmed cross-validation — different benchmark versions, different test sets, one of the two numbers figure-only.
- **Hallucination in disordered regions** is a new failure mode introduced specifically *by* switching to a generative diffusion process (AF2, non-generative, didn't have this problem) — the model can invent plausible-looking structure in genuinely unstructured regions. AF3 mitigates (not eliminates) this via cross-distillation on AlphaFold-Multimer v2.3 predictions, whose disordered regions are typically long extended loops rather than compact structures — "teaching" AF3 to mimic that ribbon-like behavior.
- **Training reveals a two-speed learning curve:** local/intrachain metrics (protein, DNA, RNA, ligand internal geometry) reach 97% of their eventual value within the first ~20,000 training steps; interface metrics (protein-protein, protein-ligand) take far longer — protein-protein interface LDDT doesn't pass the 97% mark until ~60,000 steps. Three sequential training stages with increasing crop size (384→640→768 tokens) are used, with fine-tuning specifically boosting protein-protein interfaces most.
- **Self-disclosed limitation with direct relevance to the Boltz-2/generative-pipeline thread:** AF3 predicts **static structures only**, and multiple random seeds do **not** approximate a solution ensemble — explicitly demonstrated with cereblon (an E3 ubiquitin ligase), where AF3 predicts the same closed conformation for both its known open (apo) and closed (ligand-bound) states, missing the real conformational change entirely. This is precisely the gap Boltz-2's MD-ensemble training and method-conditioning were built to address — a direct, traceable problem→solution link between these two papers already in this project.
- **Antibody-antigen specifically flagged as a persistently hard case** requiring far more sampling to resolve than other interaction types (accuracy keeps improving out to 1,000 seeds, unlike other molecule classes) — the same weak spot both Boltz-1 and Boltz-2 independently report still lagging AF3 on, suggesting this is a genuinely hard, not merely under-sampled-in-that-one-paper, problem for the whole diffusion-based co-folding approach. Two later papers now put a number on AF3's antibody-antigen ceiling on the same public benchmark: Protenix-v1 reports AF3 at 48.75% DockQ success on the FoldBench antibody-antigen subset (§4) and ESMC/ESMFold2 reports 47%±2% on 172 FoldBench antibody-antigen targets (§7) — two teams, two evaluation pipelines, landing within ~2 points of each other, and both then reporting a model that beats it. Note the qualification: Protenix-v1 takes AF3's numbers from the official FoldBench release, so the two are not fully independent re-runs of AF3 itself.
- **Access posture, a real discontinuity from AF2:** AF2 fully open-sourced code and weights. AF3 code is **not released**; only a non-commercial-use web server and algorithmic pseudocode are provided. This closed posture is the direct, stated motivating gap behind Boltz-1, OpenFold3-preview, and Protenix-v1 all existing as separate reproduction efforts — worth stating plainly since it's the concrete reason this doc has three "AF3, reproduced" branches rather than one canonical open implementation.

**AF3's closure produced three open reproduction efforts.** Two of them outgrew the label and get their own sections — Boltz-1 (§3) and Protenix (§4), both of which now make claims AF3 cannot answer. The third stays here, because it is the only one still pursuing fidelity as its stated goal:
- **OpenFold3-preview** (AlQuraishi Lab/Columbia + OpenFold Consortium, **no DOI — none exists**): **not a published paper, and deliberately not in `refs.bib`** (map-only, per `literature/map.md`) — a research preview distributed via GitHub (aqlaboratory/openfold-3), documented by technical-report PDFs rather than peer-reviewed publication, currently on its second iteration ("preview-2"). Stated goal is a **bitwise reproduction** of AF3 — full open weights, full open training data (including a reproduction of AF3's MGnify-based 13M-sequence distillation set), full open training code, Apache 2.0 licensed for both academic and commercial use — explicitly the "faithful reproduction" philosophy, not "reproduce and improve." Benchmarked against AF3 itself, FoldBench, and "Runs and Poses" on monomers, protein-protein complexes, and protein-ligand complexes; the README states it's the only open model matching AF3 on monomeric RNA structures, and is explicit that **full parity with AF3 across all modalities is still an open, in-progress goal**, not a completed claim. Given the source here is a GitHub README plus unreviewed technical-report PDFs rather than a paper, treat this as lower-confidence than even our usual search-and-memory baseline until we actually read a technical report directly.

## 3. Boltz-1 → Boltz-2 — the open reproduction that became a platform

**Boltz-1 (Wohlwend, Corso, Passaro, Getz et al., MIT, bioRxiv Nov 2024 / rev. May 2025, `10.1101/2024.11.19.624167`).** Stated goal: *"the first fully commercially accessible open-source model reaching AlphaFold3 reported levels of accuracy"* — MIT license on weights, training code, inference code, datasets and benchmarks. Distinct from OpenFold3's fidelity-first philosophy: Boltz-1 reproduces AF3's framework and then deliberately diverges wherever it thinks AF3 got something wrong.

**Data and training.**
- PDB up to **2021-09-30** (AF3's cutoff), resolution ≥ 9 Å, Biological Assembly 1, AF3's cleaning rules. MSAs from `colabfold_search`/MMseqs2 (`uniref30_2302`, `colabfold_envdb_202108`) — the same pipeline family Protenix uses, which is part of why §4's comparisons are clean.
- **68K training steps at batch 128**: 53K at 384 tokens / 3456 atoms drawing equally from PDB and the ~270K-structure **OpenFold distillation set**, then 15K PDB-only at 512 tokens / 4608 atoms. Against AF3's ~150K steps at batch 256, the paper claims **roughly a quarter of the compute** and attributes the gap to its own modifications. Note the dependency: the open AF2 reproduction (OpenFold) supplies the distillation data for the open AF3 reproduction.
- **A released benchmark split**, offered explicitly to fix the absence of a standard: 553 validation and 593 test structures, clustered at 40% sequence identity, test set post-2023-01-13 and filtered so no protein chain shares a cluster with training and no ligand exceeds 0.8 Tanimoto to a training ligand. This is the split Boltz-1's own headline numbers are computed on.
- **Dense MSA pairing** (Algorithm 3): taxonomy-based pairing for multimers that preserves MSA density, since model cost scales linearly in MSA rows.
- **Unified cropping** (Algorithm 4): a single algorithm that interpolates between spatial and contiguous cropping via a "neighborhood size" sampled uniformly in 0–40 tokens per training example, rather than AF3's use of two separate strategies.
- **Robust pocket conditioning** — the design-relevant one. AF3 needed a *separate fine-tuned model* and assumed the user knows every residue within 6 Å of the ligand. Boltz-1 keeps one unified model, reveals pocket information in 30% of training iterations with the number of revealed residues drawn from a geometric distribution, and extends the mechanism to **polymer binders** (proteins, nucleic acids), not just small molecules. That last point is what makes it epitope conditioning rather than pocket conditioning.

**Architecture and procedure changes, and what they were reacting to.**
- **MSA module reordering** (AF3 Algorithm 8): `OuterProductMean` is moved after `MSATransition` so that single-representation updates propagate into the pair representation within the same block. Boltz-1 notes in a footnote that the Ligo Biosciences AF3 reimplementation converged on the same change concurrently.
- **DiffusionTransformer residual connections** (AF3 Algorithm 23). AF3's published update is `a ← AttentionPairBias(a) + ConditionedTransitionBlock(a)`; Boltz-1 argues this both lacks residual connections and prevents the attention output feeding the transition block in the same layer, and replaces it with the standard two-step residual form. A footnote states plainly that differences like this *"may simply be the result of reporting mistakes"* in AF3's manuscript.
- **Diffusion loss weighting.** Boltz-1 uses (t̂² + σ_data²)/(t̂ × σ_data)² per the EDM framework (Karras et al. 2022) *"rather than"* AF3's Section 3.7.1 Eq. 6 form (t̂² + σ_data²)/(t̂ + σ_data)². A second concrete correction to AF3's published spec.
- **Kabsch diffusion interpolation**, the most interesting fix because it is argued from theory, not tuning. AF3 trains its non-equivariant denoiser by randomly rotating/translating inputs and computing a rigid-aligned MSE loss. Boltz-1 constructs an explicit adversarial counterexample — a model that always predicts the same structure achieves near-zero aligned training loss yet drifts out of distribution at inference, because the *unaligned* interpolation between noisy and denoised coordinates can land far from either. The fix is a Kabsch alignment before every interpolation step at inference. **Then the paper deflates its own result:** the effect is largest on models trained on data subsets where overfitting is likely, and *"the final Boltz-1 seems to largely [denoise] close to the projection making the Kapsch alignment not critical."* A theoretically real failure mode that the shipped model mostly avoids anyway — exactly the kind of self-limiting report worth crediting.
- **Confidence model, rebuilt — the piece that matters most for design filtering.** AF3 trains a 4-layer PairFormer confidence head alongside the trunk with gradients stopped. Boltz-1 instead trains the confidence model *separately* and gives it the entire trunk architecture — `AtomAttentionEncoder`, `MSAModule`, and a **48-layer** `PairFormerModule` — initialized from the trained trunk's EMA weights with all new layers zero-initialized so as not to perturb the pretrained representation. The stated inspiration is LLM reward-model fine-tuning. It additionally consumes the denoising trajectory: token representations from every reverse-diffusion step are aggregated by a time-conditioned recurrent block and concatenated into the confidence model's inputs. Since ipTM/pLDDT from these models *are* the success criterion in most binder pipelines (§1.1), a confidence head with 48 layers and trajectory awareness rather than 4 layers on final representations is a substantive difference for Level-1 filtering, not an implementation detail.

**Boltz-steering → Boltz-1x.** Motivated by direct visual inspection: whole chains placed on top of one another (AF3 reports the same), plus steric clashes, wrong bond lengths and angles, inverted chirality, non-planar aromatics — the failure class PoseBusters was built to detect, cited as such. The method tilts the diffusion distribution by a physics-inspired potential using **Feynman-Kac steering** (Singhal et al. 2025): flat-bottom potentials on chirality, stereochemistry, planarity, internal geometry, steric clash, chain overlap and covalent bonds, enforced by **SMC resampling plus backward universal guidance**, not merely by gradient updates. The paper positions this explicitly against the alternatives: AF3's approach of sampling many structures and filtering on physical heuristics *"will be unable to bias the model towards the rare conformer that satisfies the constraint"* when the constraint is rarely satisfied at all; RFdiffusion applies gradient updates on the x₀ prediction for symmetry and contact potentials — a direct citation-level bridge back to the RFdiffusion report already in this project.

**Claims vs. evidence.**
- **"AF3-level accuracy, openly"** — supported. Boltz-1 ran AF3 and Chai-1 **itself**, with the same precomputed MSAs (up to 16,384 sequences), 200 sampling steps, 10 recycles, 5 outputs, on 541 test structures and 66 CASP15 targets. Differences across mean LDDT, DockQ>0.23 and LDDT-PLI fall within confidence intervals. This in-house, matched-input protocol is why Boltz-1's cross-model numbers are the most directly comparable in this corpus.
- **Physical validity is the standout result, and the direction matters.** The paper reports that **57% of Boltz-1's top-1 poses *fail* the physical checks** (so ~43% pass); **AF3 ~58% pass; Chai-1 ~27% pass; Boltz-1x 97% pass**, with accuracy held flat. An earlier draft of this doc read the 57% as a pass rate and inverted the finding. Three consequences: Boltz-1x's jump is over the whole field, not just its own baseline; ~58% is this doc's only independent, non-self-reported physical-validity measurement of AF3; and Chai-1 is the weakest of the three on aggregate physical plausibility despite its favorable self-reported clash rate (§6).
- **Self-disclosed limitation:** architectural ablations were run on a smaller model than the released one.

**Boltz-2 (Passaro, Corso, Wohlwend et al., MIT/Valence/Recursion, bioRxiv June 2025, `10.1101/2025.06.14.659707`).** Same structural backbone; the paper is candid that co-folding gains are modest. The contribution is a new capability plus a controllability layer.

- **Structure prediction, honestly bounded.** Boltz-2 *"matches or moderately improves over"* Boltz-1, with the largest gains on RNA chains and DNA-protein complexes — precisely the modalities where distillation data was most augmented, which the paper reads as evidence that distillation is the lever where experimental data is thin. Against others it *"edg[es] the other commercially available models Chai-1 and ProteinX [Protenix], but lag[s] a bit behind AlphaFold3."* On the antibody benchmark it narrows the gap to AF3 *"while still lagging behind."* The limitations section is blunter still: the model *"does not significantly deviate from the structure prediction performance of its predecessors"* because the training data, architecture and failure modes are largely unchanged, and it *"still often fails to capture large conformational changes, such as those that can be induced by binding"* — the same defect AF3 self-disclosed with cereblon (§2), unresolved one generation later.
- **A strong applied result:** on the Polaris-ASAP competition (SARS-CoV-2 and MERS-CoV main protease ligand poses), the top six entries were all fine-tuned Boltz-1 or AF3 models; Boltz-2 beats them **out of the box, with no fine-tuning and no physics relaxation**.
- **Affinity module — the actual new capability.** A PairFormer restricted to protein-ligand and intra-ligand interactions, on a frozen trunk with detached gradients, with a binary binder/decoy head and a continuous affinity head trained on curated PubChem/ChEMBL/BindingDB data. Evidence: Pearson 0.66 on the 4-target FEP+ subset (beating all inexpensive physics and ML baselines, approaching full free-energy simulation at >1000× lower cost); competitive with OpenFE on its 876-compound hit-to-lead set; and on **CASP16's blind affinity challenge** (140 protein-ligand pairs, two targets) it *"outperforms all top-ranking participants by a clear margin"* run out-of-the-box while participants had weeks and custom tooling.
- **Virtual screening:** on MF-PCBA, Boltz-2 nearly doubles average precision over prior ML approaches and reaches an enrichment factor of **18.4 at the 0.5% threshold**, beating docking **and ipTM**. That last comparison is the one binder-design reports should carry forward: a trained affinity head outperforms the confidence-score filtering that Level-1 pipelines rely on by default.
- **Unusually candid self-criticism:** on eight blinded internal Recursion assays the model clears Pearson > 0.55 on **only 3 of 8**, with the paper stating it includes the result *"as a reminder that strong performance on public benchmarks does not always immediately translate to all complexities of real-world drug discovery."*
- **Dynamics:** MD-ensemble conditioning and B-factor supervision give better RMSF correlation than Boltz-1, BioEmu and AlphaFlow, but the paper concedes it *"does not significantly deviate from"* those specialized baselines, and that BioEmu/AlphaFlow match the reference conformational diversity more closely.
- **Controllability — the most complete conditioning system in this doc**, and the reason Boltz-2 matters to binder design beyond affinity. Experimental-**method** conditioning (X-ray / EM / NMR / MD / AF2-distilled / Boltz-1-distilled) supplied as a single-token feature and shown to shift the output distribution. **Multimeric template** conditioning — where AF3 and Chai-1 accept only single-chain templates and neither enforces them, Boltz-2 groups templates by PDB ID so chains are visible to one another, and adds a steering potential that *hard-enforces* a template over any subset of a chain (a pocket, a domain, a loop). **Contact and pocket** constraints as pairwise features with a one-hot relation type and Fourier-embedded distance in 4–20 Å, plus a time-dependent steering potential that anneals from "any pair within the cutoff" to "all pairs within the cutoff." The paper's own comparison: AF3's public model supports no distance constraints at all, Boltz-1 supports pocket conditioning but at most one restraint, Chai-1 supports pocket and contact with flexible cutoffs — Boltz-2 supports all of it *and* can enforce rather than merely suggest.
- **Stated architectural gap:** the affinity module has no explicit handling of cofactors, ions, water, or multimeric binding partners, and depends entirely on the predicted pose being right — *"if the model fails to identify the correct pocket ... downstream affinity predictions are unlikely to be reliable."*

## 4. Protenix-v1 → Protenix-v2

- **Protenix-v1** (ByteDance Seed, bioRxiv preprint posted 9 February 2026, `10.64898/2026.02.05.703733` — the entry cataloged as `protenix_v1`, paper-read section).
  - **Correction to an earlier draft of this doc, stated before anything else.** The Protenix material previously in this section — six named corrections to typos/ambiguities in AF3's supplementary algorithms, a LayerNorm patch to the confidence head, "no templates and no nucleic-acid MSAs at all", 192 GPUs / ~2 weeks / 75K+15K+4K optimizer steps, the PoseBusters-V2 common-vs-non-common-ligand disaggregation, the 7PZB/7XFA memorization critique of AF3's own showcase cases, and a "Future Plan" naming MSA-free prediction as a goal — describes the **earlier Protenix AF3-reproduction technical report (2024/2025)**, a different document that is not in this corpus and has no `refs.bib` entry. None of it appears in the cataloged 2026 paper, and one part of it is flatly contradicted by that paper (templates and RNA MSAs are now core features, see below). All of it is **withdrawn from this doc** pending a direct read of that earlier report; §9's synthesis has been rewritten accordingly. What follows is the 2026 paper only.
  - **The claim, stated tightly:** the first fully open-source structure predictor to reach or exceed AF3 *under matched conditions* — same training-data cutoff (2021-09-30), same model scale, same inference budget. The paper is explicit that the point of matching is to show the open-source-vs-AF3 gap "is not fundamental," rather than to win a headline number under favorable conditions.
  - **The direction of travel is *toward* evolutionary input, not away from it** — the single most consequential fact here for this doc's central thesis. Protenix-v1's two named additions over the prior Protenix line are **protein templates** (AF3's strategy, capped at four, e-value-ranked, with a 60-day pre-release window enforced during training to prevent leakage) and **RNA MSAs** (MMseqs2 for clustering, `nhmmer` searching Rfam / RNAcentral / the Nucleotide collection). Both are reported as accuracy wins, and templates are additionally reported to make training activations more stable. The ablation is disclosed honestly: `Protenix-v1-wo-RNA-MSA` loses protein-RNA interface and RNA monomer accuracy while other domains stay roughly unchanged — i.e. the added evolutionary signal is doing real but narrowly-scoped work.
  - **Data pipeline, still divergent from AF3:** protein MSAs come from the ColabFold pipeline (uniref30 + colabfold_envdb) with UniRef taxonomy-ID pairing, rather than AF3's jackhmmer/HHblits over UniProt, BFD and MGnify; UniRef sequences that fail pairing are retained as unpaired features rather than dropped. Training corpus: ~150K curated PDB structures at the 2021-09-30 cutoff, ~13M MGnify-based monomer distillation structures (predicted with all five original AF2 parameter sets, highest-pLDDT kept, with no length or pLDDT filtering — explicitly a fix for low-quality structures in the previous Protenix distillation corpus), and ~14K AF3-procedure disorder-distillation structures which the paper reports "effectively mitigates structural hallucination in disordered regions." That last line is an independent confirmation from a second team that AF3's disorder-hallucination failure mode (§2) is real and that distillation is the working remedy for it.
  - **Training, and one disclosed architectural difference:** staged training with increasing crop sizes of 384 / 640 / 768 tokens on a 256-GPU cluster — and, unlike AF3 and Boltz-1, **the confidence head is trained jointly with the structure predictor** rather than behind a stop-gradient. The paper states the choice without ablating it, so record it as a difference, not as an argued improvement.
  - **Claim vs. evidence, FoldBench (corrected common-intersection panel):** Protenix-v1 beats AF3 on protein-protein DockQ success rate (72.70±0.76 vs. 71.73), antibody-antigen (52.31±1.43 vs. 48.75) and protein-RNA (68.46±1.43 vs. 65.22); ties on monomer lDDT (0.8857 vs. 0.8803) and protein-ligand (62.54 vs. 62.59); and **loses clearly on protein-DNA** (69.13 vs. 75.91). Restricted instead to the intersection with AF3's officially released outputs, the same asymmetry holds — AF3 keeps protein-ligand (64.90 vs. 62.79) and protein-DNA (79.18 vs. 73.29). The paper reports the losses in the same table as the wins rather than aggregating them away, which is the standard this project should hold other "matches AF3" claims to.
  - **The methodological contribution is arguably the bigger one, and it lands on how every benchmark number in this doc should be read.** FoldBench's published aggregate metrics **do not enforce a common intersection of successfully-evaluated targets**, so a model that OOMs on large complexes is effectively scored on an easier subset. The paper's worked example: Boltz-1's reported protein-protein success rate of 172/252 = 0.683 and Chai-1's 172/251 = 0.685 become 162/237 = 0.684 and 160/237 = 0.675 once both are restricted to the 237 interfaces they share — a rank flip produced purely by coverage. Separately, FoldBench subsets are small enough that a single "5 seeds × 5 samples" run of Protenix-v1 carries a 95% CI on DockQ SR spanning **49.3%–56.3%**. Any cross-paper table assembled from this doc that quotes single-run FoldBench numbers without variance is quoting inside that noise band — which includes several of the §6 comparisons.
  - **Inference-time scaling as a first-class control knob:** on the paper's own 528-complex PXM-22to25-Antibody benchmark, DockQ success rate rises approximately log-linearly from 36.01% at one seed to 42.92% at 10 seeds and 47.68% at 80 seeds. The paper's framing is that this behavior was previously observed only in AF3 and was "largely absent from prior open-source models." It is the same axis ESMC/ESMFold2 exploits in §7, arrived at from the MSA-loyal side of the split — sampling compute and evolutionary search are alternative ways to buy the same accuracy, not opposed philosophies. Stated next step: distill the high-quality sampling signal back into the base model.
  - **An independent second sighting of Boltz-2's temporal leakage:** Protenix-v1 marks its PXM-22to25-Antibody results for Boltz-2 as "partially confounded by training data overlap." ESMC/ESMFold2 independently flags the same problem on FoldBench, from an unrelated team and a different benchmark (§7). Two papers disqualifying the same model's antibody numbers on leakage grounds is genuine cross-paper corroboration — and unlike most of the corroborations in this doc, it lands on a paper already in this corpus (§5), so it should qualify how Boltz-2's benchmark claims are read here.
  - **Ligands:** on PXM-22to25-Ligand, Protenix-v1 attains the lowest ligand RMSD (2.772 Å) and the highest RMSD<2 Å success rate (72.79%) among compared models. PB-Valid is defined here as passing all 18 PoseBusters criteria used by AF3, so it is directly comparable to the physical-validity thread of §§2–8 — but it is reported as a success-rate column only, with no chirality-violation or clash-rate analysis, so this paper adds a data point to that thread without adding a diagnosis.
  - **A design-side capability disclosed almost in passing:** Protenix-v1 achieves the highest AUC and average precision on most targets of an open-source mini-binder design filtering benchmark. That is the predictor being used as a *design filter* — the same predictor-as-critic role ESMFold2 plays in §7's binder campaign, and the natural bridge to the deferred Protenix-v2 / PXDesign review.
  - **Two checkpoints, honestly separated:** Protenix-v1-20250630 (cutoff 2025-06-30) ships alongside for practical use, with the paper stating outright that its strong PXM-2024 numbers "do not reflect generalization ability" because that set sits inside its training window. Its real gain concentrates on antibody-antigen, i.e. data scaling paying off in the data-sparse regime. Note this retires the earlier draft's "Protenix trains one model, unlike AF3's two" reading: Protenix-v1 also ships two, split by benchmark-alignment vs. deployment rather than by leakage-avoidance.

**Protenix-v2 (ByteDance Seed, bioRxiv preprint posted 11 April 2026, `10.64898/2026.04.10.717613` — prediction half only; the design half is deferred to the generative pass).** Titled *"Broadening the Reach of Structure Prediction **and** Biomolecular Design"*, and the split is clean enough that the prediction half stands alone here.

- **Same 2021-09-30 cutoff**, held deliberately so that both the structure-prediction and the zero-shot design results are assessed on genuinely held-out data. That keeps Protenix-v2 inside the four-model comparability set of §1.5.
- **The headline is antibody-antigen**, benchmarked on three collections — PXMeter-AB, FoldBench-AB and AF3-AB — against AlphaFold3, Boltz-1, **OpenFold3-preview2**, and Protenix-v1. Gains over Protenix-v1 are **9–13 percentage points** at DockQ > 0.23, with *"comparably large"* gains at the strict DockQ > 0.8 threshold, i.e. the improvement is in genuine interface geometry, not marginal passes. The paper's summary: Protenix-v2 *"moves from approximate parity with AlphaFold3 to a clear leading position in antibody-antigen modeling."*
- **The inference-scaling loop closes.** Protenix-v1 named "distill the high-quality sampling signal back into the base model" as its next step (above). Protenix-v2 reports that **at 5 seeds it already exceeds Protenix-v1 at 1000 seeds**, and outperforms competitors across the whole 1–1000 seed range. This is the clearest evidence in the corpus that sampling compute and model quality are substitutable along a predictable curve — the same trade ESMFold2 exploits from the MSA-free side (§7) and OpenDDE formalizes as a scaling law (§5).
- **A genuine advance on the physical-validity throughline: the metric itself was incomplete.** Protenix-v2 observes that models scoring well on PoseBusters still produce chemically implausible geometry — twisted amide groups, distorted aromatic rings — and extends the criterion in PXMeter v1.1.0 with checks on sp2-center planarity, amide planarity, and sp3-center *non*-planarity. Ground-truth structures pass these at near 100%, which is the justification for adding them. Its **TFG (training-free guidance)** variants, credited to Boltz-1x, projected diffusion and the SHAKE constraint algorithm from MD, impose chirality, planarity, torsion and distance constraints during generation. Joint success rates (pocket-aligned ligand RMSD < 2 Å **and** validity) on PXM-22to25-Ligand, 623 entries / 250 clusters:

  | Model | PoseBusters criterion | Revised criterion |
  |---|---|---|
  | Boltz-1 | 35.56 | 34.38 |
  | Boltz-1x | 55.59 | 53.96 |
  | Protenix-v1 | 48.37 | 48.14 |
  | Protenix-v1-TFG | 61.11 | 61.11 |
  | Protenix-v2 | 50.42 | 49.22 |
  | Protenix-v2-TFG | 60.53 | 60.46 |
  | Boltz-2 † | 51.31 | 51.25 |
  | Boltz-2x † | 63.51 | 62.86 |

  († training-set overlap with this evaluation set; shown for reference only.) Two things to carry forward. First, the largest gaps under the revised criterion are exactly the newly added checks — sp2 planarity and sp3 non-planarity — and the case studies show **Boltz-2x**, the best model on the original criterion, failing them. Steering fixes what it is told to fix. Second, do not confuse these numbers with Boltz-1x's 97% in §3: that is a validity-only pass rate on Boltz-1's own test set, while this is a joint accuracy-**and**-validity rate on a different set. Same throughline, incompatible denominators.
- **Design-side capability, noted and deferred:** target-conditioned generation supporting both epitope-specific and site-agnostic design across miniproteins, VHH and Fv formats, with per-CDR length control and scaffold specification; 100% target-level success in novelty-controlled VHH-Fc campaigns, hit rates to 48%, and GPCR hit rates of 16–88%. That belongs to the generative report, but note what it implies for this one: the antibody-antigen prediction gains and the design results are reported as one causal chain, the predictor improvement being the stated basis for the design improvement.

## 5. OpenDDE — folding as the entry point to a discovery engine

**OpenDDE (Aureka AI Research, arXiv 2026, `10.48550/arXiv.2607.03787`).** Easy to misfile as a design pipeline given the name; it is not. Its own limitations section is explicit: OpenDDE *"should be viewed as the folding-centered foundation of an extensible drug-discovery engine, **not as a complete drug-discovery system**."* Every result in the paper is co-folding accuracy, scaling behavior, or architecture. Design appears only as an architectural claim, not a campaign.

- **The framing question is the interesting part.** The paper opens by noting that *"incremental improvements in co-folding accuracy have begun to slow"* and asks whether current tokenization, data curation, architectures and inference procedures are approaching practical bottlenecks — then answers with scale rather than a new inductive bias. Positioned as the first Apache-licensed all-atom model reported to reach the accuracy level of **IsoDDE**, Isomorphic Labs' closed system, and built explicitly on top of Protenix-v1 and OpenFold3.
- **Architecture:** "atomic latent reasoning" — iterative refinement of local geometry, chemical context and interface representations *before* all-atom generation. Prediction and design are unified as one conditional denoising problem: known atoms are fixed as context, target atoms are noised and denoised, and structure prediction is simply the case where nothing is fixed. That makes OpenDDE a Level-0 design platform architecturally, with no design results yet to evaluate.
- **The most complete third-party head-to-head in this corpus.** OpenDDE benchmarks AlphaFold3, Chai-1, Boltz-1, OpenFold3, Protenix-v1, **ESMFold2** and itself on three antibody-antigen collections, under both ranked and oracle selection. This is the only place in the corpus where the fully MSA-emancipated model is run head-to-head against the whole AF3 cluster by a neutral-ish third party. Ranked, DockQ > 0.23: OpenDDE **51.0%** on PXMeter-AB, **70.0%** on FoldBench-AB, **66.4%** on 2026ARK-AB (a fresh 164-complex / 159-cluster set the authors curate from post-release structures using PXMeter's protocol, 40% sequence-identity clustering, 80% coverage).
- **The spread on the newest benchmark is the finding.** On 2026ARK-AB: OpenDDE 66.4%, **ESMFold2 51.0%**, Protenix-v1 50.0%, Boltz-1 25.0%, OpenFold3 21.9%, **Chai-1 16.7%**. ESMFold2 is named the strongest baseline on PXMeter-AB and FoldBench-AB too. Two things follow. First, ESMFold2's antibody-antigen claim (§7) now has third-party corroboration on data neither team curated — the single most important cross-check in this doc, because it is the MSA-free model holding up against MSA-based ones on the modality where MSAs are weakest. Second, the 3–4× spread between the best and worst open models on *recent, low-homology* antibody targets is far larger than the near-ties reported on older benchmarks, which says the older benchmarks are saturated and that model choice matters much more for design work than the headline tables suggest.
- **Oracle-vs-ranked gap, and what it means for filtering.** Oracle selection lifts OpenDDE to 65.9 / 81.9 / 80.1% against ranked 51.0 / 70.0 / 66.4%. The paper reads this as latent capacity that better confidence estimation could unlock. For binder design the reading is sharper and less comfortable: **roughly 15 points of achievable accuracy are being lost to ranking**, which is the same confidence machinery that Level-1 pipelines use to decide which designs to synthesize. Confidence estimation, not generation, is the binding constraint.
- **A cross-model scaling law.** OpenDDE plots antibody-antigen performance against two estimates of training scale — tokens (steps × batch × crop) and tokens × trainable parameters — for AlphaFold3, Protenix-v1, Protenix-v2, SeedFold (no DOI — not cataloged), ESMFold2, OpenDDE and IsoDDE (no DOI), and reports a smooth sublinear trend approaching the IsoDDE level. This is the third scaling result in the corpus (after ESMC's two fits in §7) and the only one that places every model in this doc on a single axis. It is also the closest thing available to a measurement of a closed commercial system: IsoDDE appears as a reference level on someone else's curve. Treat the fit as suggestive — the token estimates are reconstructed from published training descriptions, not reported by each team — and note the paper's own hedge that *"scale alone does not fully determine performance."*
- **Disclosed limitations, unusually forthright.** The IsoDDE comparison is constrained because IsoDDE's training recipe, data mixture and inference procedure are unpublished, so *"we cannot yet determine which differences arise from model architecture, data processing, scaling, distillation, or inference-time procedures."* And the model is deliberately protein-protein and antibody-antigen focused: preliminary analysis found *limited correlation* between protein-protein interface performance and protein-ligand pose accuracy, so the authors *"do not claim that the present model is optimized for ligand docking, virtual screening, or affinity ranking."* That negative observation — that the two regimes may need different data and inductive biases — is worth carrying forward, since much of this doc's physical-validity evidence comes from the ligand side while the binder-design interest is entirely on the protein side.

## The RoseTTAFold lineage has moved — three findings kept here

**RoseTTAFold (RF1, 2021, `10.1126/science.abj8754`) and RoseTTAFold All-Atom (RFAA, 2024, `10.1126/science.adl2528`) are now reviewed in `literature/reports/rfdiffusion_family_summary.md`**, as the origin of the lineage they actually feed. Two reasons. First, the architecture *is* the lineage: RFdiffusion is a fine-tuned RoseTTAFold and RFdiffusionAA a fine-tuned RFAA, so splitting parent from descendant breaks one story across two documents. Second, the lineage has left the predictor conversation entirely — mentions in each co-folding paper's text run AlphaFold3 25, Chai-1 11, then **Boltz-1, Boltz-2, Protenix-v1, Protenix-v2, OpenDDE and ESMC/ESMFold2 all zero**. No predictor published since 2024 benchmarks against it.

Three of its findings are load-bearing for arguments in *this* document, and are kept here in short form:

- **Chirality as architecture, the fourth response to the physical-validity problem (§9.2).** RFAA is the only model in this project that never traded equivariance for diffusion. Because its 1D and 2D tracks are reflection-invariant, it encodes (R)/(S) centers on the 3D track as the sign of angles around each center, feeding the gradient of the deviation from ideal geometry into every subsequent block — chirality built in, rather than penalized at ranking time (AF3, Protenix), steered at inference (Boltz-1x, Protenix-TFG), or projected out (Gauss-Seidel). **Caveat that keeps it unsettled:** RFAA's paper reports no PoseBusters-style validity pass rate at all, so the by-construction route is architecturally distinct but metrically unverified.
- **"Sampling helps, ranking doesn't" — first observed in 2021, still true in 2026 (§9.3a).** RF1 reported that iteratively re-templating and subsampling MSAs generated ensembles *containing* higher-accuracy models, but its confidence predictor *"was not able to consistently identify"* them. OpenDDE's ~15-point oracle-versus-ranked gap on every antibody benchmark is the same diagnosis five years later. Two papers, five years apart, on opposite sides of the MSA question.
- **The only all-atom co-folder not motivated by AF3's closure.** RFAA published 7 March 2024, two months *before* AF3 (8 May 2024) — concurrent and independent, not a response. Boltz-1, Protenix and OpenFold3 all exist because AF3 closed; RFAA does not. Worth keeping when §9.4 characterizes the open/closed dynamic.

## 6. Chai-1 — the hinge

**Chai-1 (Chai Discovery, bioRxiv Oct 2024, `10.1101/2024.10.10.615955`).** Architecturally an AF3 derivative — the paper states directly that it "largely follows" Abramson et al., with heavy reliance on pair-bias self-attention and the same overall trunk→diffusion→confidence pattern (48 pair-bias blocks in the trunk, a diffusion structure-prediction stage run across 4 recycles, a 4-block confidence head). Like Protenix and unlike AF3 itself, Chai-1 trains **one single model** with one cutoff (2021-01-12) rather than AF3's two-cutoff strategy.

- **The addition that earns it "hinge" status:** a parallel input track of residue-level embeddings from a 3B-parameter pretrained protein language model (ESM-lineage), running *alongside* the ordinary MSA track rather than replacing it. The paper's own framing of the motivation is precise and worth preserving: prior protein LMs had already shown value for single-chain folding, but "have yet to demonstrate actionable performance on multimeric prediction or protein-ligand interactions" — Chai-1's contribution is showing the LM track generalizes to exactly those harder, multi-entity cases. Mechanically, modified residues get mapped to their canonical parent (or an unknown-residue token) for the LM track, and non-protein tokens (DNA, RNA, ligands) simply get a mask token — the LM track only ever "sees" protein sequence.
- **A second genuine addition, distinct from anything in AF3/Boltz/Protenix as read so far:** three explicit **constraint feature types** — pocket (token + chain + distance threshold), contact (token pair + distance threshold), and docking (coarse binned inter-chain distances) — trained with per-feature and per-chain/token dropout so the model doesn't over-rely on them, and explicitly designed to mimic real experimental modalities: epitope mapping, cross-linking mass spectrometry, hydrogen-deuterium exchange. This is a richer, more experimentally-grounded conditioning system than AF3's single pocket-conditioning mode (which Boltz-1's discussion characterized as needing two separate models and full pocket specification) — Chai-1 handles partial, graded, and inter-chain conditioning in one unified mechanism.

**Claims vs. evidence, organized by task (all comparisons run by the Chai team themselves, except where noted):**

- **Protein-ligand (PoseBusters V1, n=427 after the Chai team dropped PDB 7D6O):** Chai-1 77.05% vs. AF3's self-reported 76.34% (comparable) vs. RoseTTAFold-All-Atom's 42% (far behind). A genuinely important caveat the paper states directly: **AF3 and ESM3 could not be run by the Chai team at all**, due to commercial-use license restrictions — the AF3 ligand number is taken from AF3's own publicly released predictions, not an independent re-run. This is the same closed-AF3-access problem already flagged in the AF3 and Boltz-1 sections, but here it's more severe: it isn't "we had to reproduce AF3 ourselves," it's "we structurally cannot benchmark against it directly for most tasks," which is why Chai-1 has no protein-protein, antibody, or monomer comparison to AF3 anywhere in the paper — only to AlphaFold-Multimer 2.3 and RoseTTAFold2NA.
- **Multimeric protein prediction (n=2,362 interfaces / 929 clusters, low-homology, post-cutoff):** Chai-1 with MSA beats AF2.3 significantly (DockQ success 0.751 vs. 0.677, p=6.24×10⁻¹⁰). The central MSA-emancipation result: **Chai-1 in single-sequence mode (0.698) is statistically indistinguishable from AF2.3 with full MSAs (0.677)** — the paper states this is the first model to offer high-accuracy multimer folding with no MSA requirement at all, not just a degraded fallback mode.
- **Antibody-protein subset (n=268 interfaces, 122 clusters):** Chai-1-with-MSA beats AF2.3 by an even larger margin (p=3.25×10⁻⁵) than the general multimer case. More strikingly, **Chai-1's single-sequence and full-MSA modes perform similarly to each other** on antibodies specifically, and both beat AF2.3-with-MSA. The paper's own mechanistic explanation is worth preserving exactly: antibodies are hypervariable, so there is simply less exploitable coevolutionary signal available in an MSA for this class of sequence in the first place — MSA-emancipation isn't succeeding here because the LM track is unusually good, but because the thing it's substituting for was never carrying much signal to begin with.
- **A real, honestly-reported negative result that qualifies the whole MSA-emancipation story:** on **protein monomers**, single-sequence Chai-1 *underperforms* AF2.3 significantly (p=4.46×10⁻¹⁶) — the LM-track substitution strategy that works well for multimers and antibodies does not transfer to ordinary monomer folding, where genuine, rich coevolutionary signal exists and evidently still matters. With full MSA, Chai-1 does beat AF2.3 by a small but significant margin overall (p=7.21×10⁻¹⁰), and the more informative finding is *where*: on the 14/69 CASP15 targets AF2.3 handles worst (LDDT<0.75), Chai-1-with-MSA is clearly better (0.643 vs. 0.552, p=3.66×10⁻⁴) — Chai-1's real edge is concentrated on AF2.3's hardest cases, not a uniform lift. For reference, ESM3's 98B-parameter model reaches 0.801 average LDDT on the full 70-target CASP15 set, versus Chai-1's 0.849 and AF2.3's 0.843 — but Chai-1 and AF2.3 are scored on the 69 targets Chai-1 could actually run (T1169 excluded for inference time), so this is a near-identical rather than an identical set, and the Chai team could not run ESM3 themselves for licensing reasons. Directionally it is still a useful data point on how far a very large single-sequence-only LM trailed MSA-based methods on pure monomer prediction as of 2024 — a gap ESMC/ESMFold2 later closes on complexes but never claims to have closed via ESM3 (§7).
- **Nucleic acids:** Chai-1 has **no MSA support for nucleic acid chains at all** — not a toggle, an architectural absence — yet performs comparably to RoseTTAFold2NA (which does have full nucleic-acid MSA access) on both protein-nucleotide interfaces and CASP15 RNA. AF3 reportedly reaches 0.473 LDDT on 8 CASP15 RNA targets (self-reported, unverified by Chai's team for the same licensing reason as above), suggesting AF3's nucleic-acid MSA usage may still give it a real edge over both MSA-free/non-MSA nucleic methods here — worth treating as a single-source, not cross-validated, data point.
- **Epitope-conditioning experiment (antibody-antigen specifically):** blind baseline 35% DockQ-acceptable; a single 15Å contact restraint lifts this to 57%; four sampled epitope residues more than doubles success across all quality thresholds. The honestly-reported ceiling: even with this conditioning, **high-quality (DockQ≥0.8) predictions remain rare (4–8%)** — the paper states plainly that high-quality antibody-antigen prediction "remains a generally challenging task," a fourth independent confirmation (after AF3, Boltz-1, Boltz-2) that antibody-antigen is a genuinely hard case for this whole architectural family, not an artifact of any one paper's sampling budget.
- **Physical validity (PoseBusters detailed checks):** Chai-1 shows the same chirality-violation weakness AF3 self-reports — a fourth paper independently landing on this exact inherited failure mode. The paper also reports a genuinely distinct result — **a *lower* inter-molecular clash rate than AF3, with the authors stating they needed no clash penalty for sample ranking at all**, unlike AF3, Protenix and Boltz-1, all of which add one. **An earlier draft of this doc treated that as a favorable, unexplained outlier; the corpus does not support that reading.** It is a self-reported, clash-specific claim from Chai-1's own Figure S1, and it is contradicted on the broader measure by the one independent benchmark available: Boltz-1 runs the full physical-validity check suite on all three models and finds only **~27% of Chai-1's poses pass every check, against ~58% for AF3** (§3) — i.e. on overall physical plausibility Chai-1 is the *worst* of the three, not the best. The two results are reconcilable (few clashes, many other violations — chirality among them, which Chai-1 itself concedes), but the honest summary is: Chai-1 self-reports an advantage on one physical axis and is independently measured as behind on the aggregate. Do not carry the clash number forward as evidence that Chai-1 escaped AF3's physical-validity inheritance.
- **Self-disclosed limitations:** (1) chains individually correct but placed in the wrong relative orientation without added contact information — the same class of failure Boltz-1 called hallucination/global-arrangement error, and precisely what Chai-1's own constraint features are designed to fix. (2) A distinctive, specific finding not seen elsewhere in this doc yet: Chai-1 is **highly sensitive to modified residues** — removing a modification or substituting the unmodified parent amino acid can cause large structural changes, because the model was trained explicitly on modified structures and effectively treats the modified and unmodified sequence as different inputs entirely, not as the same input with an incidental annotation.
- **A direct methodological caveat the authors state themselves:** their evaluation set construction follows AF3's published procedure but **cannot exactly reproduce AF3's actual evaluation set** — so Chai-1's protein-protein, antibody, and nucleic-acid numbers are explicitly *not* directly comparable to AF3's own reported numbers on the same tasks, even where both papers report a number for what looks like the same benchmark. Worth remembering when building any cross-paper table from this doc: Boltz-1's numbers are more directly comparable to AF3's (same MSA sizes, same models run in-house) than Chai-1's are.
- **Server vs. paper, disclosed transparently:** the public Chai-1 Lab web server uses a faster, cruder MSA pipeline (single jackhmmer iteration, no Uniclust30+BFD, no templates) than the paper's benchmarked pipeline (3 iterations, full database set) — spot-checked to differ by <1Å RMSD on average (μ=0.345Å) but explicitly flagged as a real accuracy/speed trade-off between the free public tool and the paper's reported numbers.

## 7. ESM2/ESMFold1 → ESMC/ESMFold2 — emancipation from MSA, with one disclosed exception

**ESM2 / ESMFold1 (Lin et al., *Science* 2023, `10.1126/science.ade2574`).** The founding claim of the whole lineage: atomic-resolution protein structure "emerges" purely from scaling a masked-language-model objective on sequence alone, with no MSA, no templates, and no external database search at inference time. The paper's evidentiary strategy is a controlled scaling sweep — a single family, **ESM-2, trained at eight scales from 8 million to 15 billion parameters**, all evaluated the same way — which is what makes its central claim more than a one-off result: it is a dose-response curve.

- **The scaling result itself.** Perplexity (a token-level fidelity measure, 1=perfect, 20=random-guessing over 20 amino acids) falls monotonically with scale — 10.45 at 8M params, 6.37 at 15B — with **no observed plateau** across four orders of magnitude. This is tied directly to structure: across the eight model scales, validation perplexity correlates with CASP14 TM-score at **r = −0.99** and with CAMEO TM-score at **r = −1.00**; long-range unsupervised contact precision (extracted via a linear probe on attention maps, not a trained structure head) correlates with CASP14 TM-score at **r = 0.96** and CAMEO at **r = 0.99**. The correlation between contact-precision change and perplexity change across scale transitions is NDCG = 0.87. These are unusually tight cross-metric correlations for a field this noisy, and the paper's whole "understanding causes structure" argument rests on them holding together this cleanly.
- **A specific, disclosed asymmetry in *where* scale helps.** Proteins with many evolutionary relatives in the training set (high MSA depth by proxy) show steep gains at small scale that saturate early; proteins with few relatives keep improving all the way to 15B. This is the paper's own explanation for why scaling doesn't uniformly help everything: at 15B parameters the model is still closing the gap specifically on the *hard*, low-homology tail, which is exactly the tail an MSA-based method also struggles with (shared weak spot, different cause).
- **ESMFold architecture:** ESM-2 (the frozen or jointly-trained LM) feeds an 8-block folding trunk (alternating sequence/pair representation updates, borrowing triangular updates from the AF2 lineage) into an equivariant-transformer structure module, with 3 recycling steps — a **major architectural simplification** relative to AF2/RF1's MSA-row/column attention machinery, trained with the same FAPE-style losses as AlphaFold, on ~325,000 experimental clusters plus ~12 million AlphaFold2-predicted structures used as distillation targets.
- **Ablations with measured impact (fig. S3):** removing the language model entirely (keeping only the folding trunk) drops CAMEO LDDT from 0.74 to 0.58; removing the folding trunk (LM + structure module only, no pair-representation processing) drops it to 0.66. By contrast, removing single structure-module blocks, disabling recycling, dropping the AlphaFold2-distillation targets, or removing triangular updates each cost only 0.01–0.04 LDDT — a clean, quantified ranking of which architectural pieces are load-bearing (language model » folding trunk » everything else).
- **Claims vs. evidence.** ESMFold reaches TM-score 0.83 on CAMEO and 0.68 on CASP14, versus AlphaFold2 (full MSA+templates) at 0.88/0.85 — a real, disclosed accuracy gap, not parity. But the gap tracks perplexity almost entirely: on the 18/51 CASP14 proteins where ESM-2 achieves perplexity <7, ESMFold matches AlphaFold2; Pearson correlation between perplexity and TM-score is −0.52 (CAMEO) and −0.71 (CASP14). The paper's own framing is precise and worth preserving: ESMFold's failures are not random noise, they are the language model *telling you*, via its own uncertainty on a given sequence, when it doesn't have enough to go on — the first appearance in this lineage of the "LM confidence predicts folding accuracy" idea that ESMC's paper later sharpens into a quantitative MSA-allocation rule (see below). On complexes, ESMFold reaches the same qualitative DockQ bucket as AlphaFold-Multimer on 53.2% of chain pairs from 2,978 recent multimeric PDB entries — a real capability given the model was never trained on complexes at all, but well short of a solved problem.
- **Speed and the metagenomic payoff.** 14.2 s per 384-residue structure on a single V100 (6× faster than one AlphaFold2 model; up to ~60× on short sequences), with the MSA search step (>10 min at AF2/RF1's published sensitivity) eliminated entirely. This is what let the same team fold **>617 million MGnify90 sequences in 2 weeks on ~2,000 GPUs**, yielding ~225 million high-confidence structures (pLDDT>0.7, pTM>0.7), 76.8% of which sit below 90% sequence identity to anything in UniRef90, and 12.6% of which have no good structural match to any known PDB entry either — the ESM Metagenomic Atlas. (The Atlas is a database, not a model, and is out of scope for this doc per the exclusion note above; it's mentioned here only because it is ESMFold1's own headline demonstration of what single-sequence speed buys.)

**ESMC + ESMFold2 (Candido, Hayes, Derry, Rao, Lin et al., … Rives, bioRxiv June 2026, `10.64898/2026.06.03.729735`, "Language Modeling Materializes a World Model of Protein Biology").** Affiliation, as the paper itself states it: Biohub, with a second footnote covering authors whose work was performed "at Biohub or EvolutionaryScale" — not the EvolutionaryScale-first attribution an earlier draft used. Five co-first authors, Rives last. This is explicitly a two-model release built the same way ESM2/ESMFold1 was — a scaled-up sequence LM (ESMC) plus a folding head trained on its frozen representations (ESMFold2) — but with a materially different trunk design and, critically, a resolved answer to a question this doc previously flagged as unconfirmed.

- **The MSA question, now settled precisely.** ESMFold2 genuinely treats MSA as *optional* in the architectural sense, not merely "usually skippable": there is a real, separable 4-layer MSA encoder (outer-product-mean into the pair stream, pair-weighted MSA averaging, triangle multiplication — triangle *attention* was tested and dropped as unhelpful) whose contribution is added into the recurrent trunk's pair representation alongside the ESMC-derived contribution. The **ESMFold2-Fast** variant has this module deleted outright (Table S6 lists its MSA-encoder depth/width as "–", not "0" or "disabled") — architectural proof the module is genuinely separable, not just switched off at inference. One correction to an earlier draft: Fast is *not* simply the full model minus its MSA encoder — it also halves the folding trunk, 24 pair-folding layers against ESMFold2's 48. So when Fast matches the full model's single-sequence score below, that is a half-depth trunk matching a full-depth one, which makes the result stronger, not a like-for-like MSA ablation. The paper's own account of *why* MSA remains useful at all is the single sharpest empirical result in this section: a pseudo-perplexity (PPPL) analysis shows MSA conditioning "provides little benefit at low [ESMC] PPL but improves DockQ markedly for higher-PPL sequences," with the single-sequence-vs-MSA DockQ gap correlating with mean ESMC perplexity of the interacting chains at **Pearson r = 0.82**. Their own words: *"the MSA evolutionary context is primarily used to compensate for the language model uncertainty, and is otherwise redundant for well-characterized sequences."* This turns ESM2/ESMFold1's qualitative "perplexity predicts failure" observation into a quantitative allocation rule — MSA search becomes a targeted rescue mechanism for exactly the sequences the LM itself flags as uncertain, rather than a universal input.
- **ESMC's scaling behavior versus ESM2.** ESMC is released at 300M/600M/6B parameters, trained on **~2.8 billion sequences** (156M from UniRef release 2023_02, 2.029B from JGI, ~621M from MGnify; the paper rounds, and states the expansion as "close to two orders of magnitude" over ESM2) — roughly two orders of magnitude beyond ESM2's ~50M-sequence UniRef50 training set — using an updated transformer recipe (RoPE, QK-layernorm, SwiGLU, µP scaling, FP8 mixed precision at 6B). A fitted scaling law relates training FLOPs to unsupervised long-range contact precision (P@L-LR = 0.115·log₁₀(FLOPs) − 1.98, R²=0.99), and the 6B production run lands within 0.007 P@L-LR of this fit — but the more consequential finding is a *qualitative* one: ESMC shows **no diminishing-returns plateau** between its mid-size and largest checkpoints the way ESM2 visibly did between 650M and 15B. Concretely, ESMC-300M (0.552 P@L-LR) is comparable to ESM2-650M (0.566) at roughly half the parameters, ESMC-600M (0.589) is statistically indistinguishable from ESM2-3B (0.587) and nearly matches ESM2-15B (0.593), and ESMC-6B (0.725) outperforms the largest ESM2 models by 0.132 P@L-LR — i.e., the same parameter budget buys more structural signal in the newer training recipe/data regime, and the ceiling ESM2's own scaling curve seemed to be approaching was a training artifact, not a fundamental limit.
- **Trunk redesign, not just a bigger LM.** ESMFold2's folding trunk replaces AF2/ESMFold1-style Evoformer blocks with a purely triangle-multiplication trunk (no attention, no sequence-representation stream at all in the pair-processing loop — the paper reports removing the sequence-wise residual stream "does not degrade performance" once compute-matched) run as a **stabilized recurrent loop** rather than a fixed-depth stack: a discretized linear-dynamical-system update constrains the loop to be contractive, which is what lets the model be trained with a randomized (Poisson, mean 3) number of loop iterations and then run with *more* loops at inference (tested to 64) as an explicit inference-time-compute knob — a different mechanism from AF3-style recycling, and the direct architectural reason ESMFold2 exposes "spend more compute per prediction" as a first-class inference-time control. Diffusion is truncated from 100 to 68 effective steps with no measured quality loss, and dense pair-bias atom attention is replaced by sliding-window attention with 3D RoPE purely for kernel-efficiency reasons.
- **Claims vs. evidence (FoldBench, all baselines re-run locally on the same pipeline).** The headline comparison is antibody-antigen prediction: ESMFold2 in **single-sequence mode reaches 50%±2%** DockQ-pass, *exceeding* AlphaFold3-with-MSA's 47%±2% on the same 172 targets — a genuine, apples-to-apples single-sequence-beats-MSA result on the class of target where AF3, Boltz-1, Boltz-2, and Chai-1 were all independently flagged elsewhere in this doc as weakest. With MSA, ESMFold2 rises to 53%±2% (55%±2% with 20 recurrent loops); ESMFold2-Fast, which has no MSA pathway at all, still reaches 50%±2%, matching the full model's single-sequence score. On protein-protein interfaces (278 of FoldBench's 279, with 8gqp excluded as a D-protein binder), single-sequence ESMFold2 reaches 70%±1%, MSA mode 76%±1%, against AF3-with-MSA's ~73% — MSA-mode ESMFold2 edges out AF3 here too, though the single-sequence mode does not. Provenance caveat: ESMFold2's own 70%/76%/84%/89% figures are stated in the paper's text, but the AF3 comparison values on this subset are readable only from Figure 2C, so quote them as approximate. On ordinary monomers, single-sequence mode reaches 84%, MSA mode 89% — the paper does not report a head-to-head AF3/AF2 monomer number in the same units, so this pairing is weaker evidence than the antibody/protein-protein comparisons. On a freshly-constructed, strictly post-training-cutoff protein-protein set built specifically to avoid a **disclosed temporal-leakage problem with Boltz-2** (Boltz-2's training cutoff postdates part of FoldBench's own evaluation window), MSA-mode ESMFold2 reaches 77%±3%, "significantly higher than Boltz-2 (70%±3%)." With single-sequence ESMFold2 at 70%±4% and ESMFold2-Fast at 66%±3.5% on the same set. **This leakage finding is now independently corroborated:** Protenix-v1 separately marks Boltz-2's antibody results on its own PXM-22to25-Antibody benchmark as confounded by training-data overlap (§4). Two unrelated teams, two different benchmarks, same disqualification — which means Boltz-2's benchmark numbers as reported in §5 should be read with that caveat attached. Inference-time-compute scaling (1,024 seeds × 5 diffusion samples) lifts antibody-antigen pass rate from 49% to 65% for the full model, and from 45% to 65% for ESMFold2-Fast — the cheaper, MSA-free variant closing the entire gap to the larger model purely by spending more sampling compute, which is a clean demonstration that the recurrent-loop/many-seeds inference knobs are doing real work, not just adding noise.
- **A second, independently fitted scaling law** ties *downstream folding accuracy* to *upstream language-model pretraining compute*, holding the folding head fixed: LDDT = 0.027·log₁₀(FLOPs) + 0.181 (R²=0.92) for monomers, and DockQ-pass-rate = 0.060·log₁₀(FLOPs) − 0.832 (R²=0.93) for protein–protein complexes, fit across intermediate checkpoints of all three ESMC sizes. This is a distinct, useful result from the first scaling law: it says the benefit of scaling the *language model itself* propagates predictably into structure accuracy even when the folding head's own architecture and training are held constant — direct support for this doc's framing that ESMC/ESMFold2 is betting the whole architecture on the LM having already learned what MSA search retrieves at inference time.
- **Self-disclosed limitations, several genuinely unflattering.** ESMC "performs worse than or similar to existing tools" — including plain BLOSUM62 and the MSA-based EVMutation — for zero-shot viral variant-effect prediction across 25 ProteinGym DMS datasets, a direct, stated negative result rather than a hedge. Zero-shot global stability (ΔG) prediction "fails to show predictive value" on structurally-distinct held-out families, even though local ΔΔG zero-shot performance is reasonable (Spearman ≈0.56). A smooth-LDDT auxiliary loss is reported as "vital for global folding accuracy, but detrimental for all-atom quality (such as interatomic clashes)" and is therefore only applied in the initial training stage — an explicit, unresolved accuracy/physical-plausibility trade-off resolved by scheduling rather than by architecture (the same genre of trade-off Boltz-1x's steering module was built to fix downstream, though ESMC's paper does not frame it that way or report a PoseBusters-style validity pass rate at all). Long-context generalization (tested via a context-parallel extension to 4k–6.5k tokens) is imperfect: of 6 large post-cutoff complexes tested, 4 reach TM-score 0.9–0.99, but 2 show "accurate local structure but large deviations in global domain arrangement" (TM-score ≈0.7–0.8) — a disclosed large-assembly failure mode distinct from anything reported for the AF3-lineage models in this doc.
- **Physical validity is a secondary concern here, not the paper's framing.** Unlike the AF3/Boltz/Protenix/Chai-1 thread running through §§2–7, this paper does not report a chirality-violation rate or a PoseBusters-style validity-check pass rate as a headline number; clash avoidance appears only as training-data filtering (excluding chains with >30% of atoms within 1.7 Å of another chain) and MolProbity is used internally to tune inference hyperparameters (loop count, diffusion steps), not reported as a benchmarked output-quality claim. The paper's entire "beyond MSA" argument is framed in accuracy (DockQ/LDDT) and speed terms — this is a genuine gap relative to the rest of this doc's physical-validity throughline, worth flagging rather than assuming resolved.
- **A conspicuous omission: ESM3.** ESM3 (`10.1126/science.ads0018`; out of scope for this project, per the map's own scoping note) is mentioned exactly twice in the ESMC paper — once in the introduction, credited only for its *generative* capability (designing a fluorescent protein highly divergent from any known natural sequence), and once as a baseline (the 1.4B "Open" checkpoint, not the full 98B model) in the viral variant-effect-prediction ablation, where it is grouped with ESMC as also underperforming. The paper never engages ESM3 on structure-prediction accuracy grounds at all — no CASP15 LDDT comparison, no reference to the ~0.801-LDDT/98B-parameter figure reported for ESM3 in Chai-1's paper. ESMC/ESMFold2 is positioned entirely against AlphaFold3, Chai-1, Protenix-v1, Boltz-1/2, and OpenFold3-preview as its structure-prediction competitive set; ESM3 is treated purely as a predecessor generative system, never as something ESMFold2 needs to beat on folding accuracy.
- **The wet-lab binder-design demonstration (headline numbers only — deep pipeline treatment deferred, per the exclusion note above).** The same paper couples ESMC (sequence prior) and ESMFold2 (structural critic) in a 150-step gradient search over continuous amino-acid probabilities to design minibinders and antibody-derived scFvs against five oncology/immunology targets (PDGFRβ, EGFR, PD-L1, CD45, CTLA-4). With a ~4.5× increase in design-pool/critic-ensemble compute, measured hit rates (BLI-validated) rose from 53.8% to 70.0% for minibinders and roughly doubled (12.1%→21.0%) for scFvs — a clean, disclosed compute-scaling result for the design side, mirroring the inference-time-scaling result on the prediction side. Validated affinities ranged from 68 pM to 70 nM; a cryo-EM structure of an EGFR-minibinder complex (3.8 Å, refined to 3.68 Å) matched the computational model at 1.204 Å RMSD; a PD-L1 minibinder blocked PD-1 signaling in a Jurkat reporter assay at IC50 = 1.6 nM, comparable to the atezolizumab-scFv positive control (IC50 = 2.6 nM). This makes ESMC/ESMFold2 — like ESMFold2 is already flagged in the project's map.md — a genuine dual-role system (predictor + design engine) in the same sense as the systems catalogued in §1.3, not merely a faster predictor; the full design-pipeline mechanics (gradient search details, critic ensembling, novelty/diversity analysis) are left for the later generative-design pass.

## 8. Reading the numbers

Every cross-model comparison in this doc rests on shared benchmarks and a shared validity suite. None of the three is a model, none deserves a section, and all three have documented problems that change how the tables above should be read.

- **PoseBusters** (Buttenschoen, Morris & Deane, *Chem. Sci.* 2024, `10.1039/D3SC04185A`) — an RDKit-based suite of chemical and geometric validity checks plus a benchmark set, and the source of the "PB-valid" metric that AF3, Chai-1, Boltz-1 and Protenix all report. Its own subject is **small-molecule docking** (DiffDock, EquiBind, Vina, Gold), and its own conclusion — that no deep-learning docking method yet beat classical tools on physical plausibility or on generalization to novel sequences — is about ligands, not protein binders. Boltz-1 cites it as the motivation for Boltz-steering. Two limits worth remembering: it is a ligand-side instrument being used to judge models this project cares about for protein-side reasons, and **Protenix-v2 demonstrates it is incomplete** — models that pass its checks still produce twisted amides and distorted aromatics (§4).
- **FoldBench** (Xu et al., *Nat. Commun.* 17(1):442, 2025, `10.1038/s41467-025-67127-3`) — the shared all-atom benchmark behind several comparisons here, and currently *not* in `refs.bib`. **Protenix-v1's critique of it is load-bearing:** FoldBench's published aggregate metrics do not enforce a common intersection of successfully-evaluated targets, so a model that runs out of memory on large complexes is scored on an easier subset. Worked example — Boltz-1's reported 172/252 = 0.683 and Chai-1's 172/251 = 0.685 become 162/237 = 0.684 and 160/237 = 0.675 on their 237 shared interfaces: a rank flip caused by coverage alone. And its subsets are small enough that a single "5 seeds × 5 samples" run of Protenix-v1 carries a 95% CI on DockQ SR of **49.3%–56.3%**.
- **PXMeter** (Ma et al., bioRxiv 2025, `10.1101/2025.07.17.664878`) — ByteDance's evaluation framework and the basis of the PXM benchmark family; v1.1.0 carries the extended validity criterion described in §4.

**Practical rules for anything built on this doc.**
1. Quote AF3, Boltz-1, Protenix-v1 and Protenix-v2 against one another freely — same 2021-09-30 cutoff. Anything reaching outside that set needs the cutoff stated.
2. **Boltz-2's benchmark numbers carry a leakage caveat from three independent sources.** ESMC/ESMFold2 flags that FoldBench's evaluation window falls inside Boltz-2's training data; Protenix-v1 marks Boltz-2's PXM-22to25-Antibody results as *"partially confounded by training data overlap"*; Protenix-v2 marks its ligand results the same way, noting the 2023 cutoff. Three teams, three benchmarks, same disqualification.
3. Never compare a validity-only pass rate to a joint accuracy-and-validity rate (§3 vs §4).
4. Prefer numbers a paper generated by running the baselines itself with matched inputs (Boltz-1, ESMFold2, OpenDDE) over numbers lifted from another paper's release (Chai-1's AF3 column, Protenix-v1's AF3 column). Both kinds appear above and are labeled where known.
5. Where a number is readable only from a figure, this doc says so. Treat those as approximate.

## 9. Synthesis, and what carries into the design reports

### 9.1 Where the MSA question stands

MSA-emancipation is not a uniform property of "add a language-model track" — it's task- and entity-dependent, and by now measurable in two independent ways. Chai-1's single-sequence mode matches or beats MSA-based baselines on multimers and especially antibodies (precisely where MSAs carry little signal to begin with, per the paper's own hypervariability explanation), but *loses* to MSA-based AF2.3 on ordinary protein monomers, where real coevolutionary signal exists and evidently still matters. ESMC/ESMFold2 arrives at the same conclusion through a different, sharper instrument: rather than splitting performance by molecule class, it splits by the language model's own per-sequence perplexity, and finds MSA value is "otherwise redundant for well-characterized sequences" once that per-sequence uncertainty is accounted for — and backs the claim with a genuine single-sequence-beats-MSA result on antibody-antigen prediction specifically (50%±2% vs. AlphaFold3-with-MSA's 47%±2%), the exact target class both papers independently flag as MSA-poor. Two independent lineages, two different diagnostic variables (molecule class vs. LM perplexity), converging on the same underlying claim is a stronger result than either paper offers alone; the open question this leaves for the corpus is whether "low exploitable MSA signal" and "high LM perplexity" are actually the same underlying quantity measured two ways, or a coincidence of these two particular test sets.


### 9.2 Physical validity: an inherited defect, patched four different ways

AF2→AF3 traded equivariance-by-construction (IPA) for a non-equivariant, purely-learned diffusion process. RFdiffusion3 inherits that same choice wholesale, and it directly produced the physical-validity failure mode — chirality violations at a self-reported 4.4%, plus persistent clashing — that everything downstream has been patching since. Four distinct responses, in ascending order of intervention:

1. **Re-rank finished samples** on a physical-validity penalty — AF3 and Protenix-v1.
2. **Steer the diffusion trajectory** with physics potentials — Boltz-1x (Feynman-Kac / SMC resampling), Protenix-v1/v2-TFG (training-free guidance, SHAKE-inspired constraints).
3. **Build chirality into the architecture** as input features on the 3D track — RFAA, the only model here that never made the equivariance trade. Its paper reports no PoseBusters-style pass rate at all, so the by-construction route remains architecturally distinct and metrically unverified.
4. **Fix the metric** — Protenix-v2, on the finding that passing PoseBusters does not imply chemical plausibility.

The one hard, non-self-reported measurement remains Boltz-1's in-house benchmark: AF3 ~58% pass, Boltz-1 ~43%, Chai-1 ~27%, Boltz-1x 97%. Chai-1's self-reported low clash rate with no clash penalty needed is not evidence it escaped the inheritance — on the aggregate check it is the worst of the three, and no paper in the corpus explains the discrepancy.

**For binder design specifically:** almost all of this evidence is ligand-side, and OpenDDE reports *limited correlation* between protein-protein interface performance and protein-ligand pose accuracy. Whether the physical-validity story transfers to protein-protein interfaces is, on this corpus, an open question rather than an established one.

### 9.3 Three findings that matter more than accuracy for the design reports

**(a) Confidence estimation, not generation, is the binding constraint.** OpenDDE's oracle selection beats its ranked selection by ~15 points on every antibody benchmark — the models already generate good interfaces they then fail to identify. RoseTTAFold reported the identical pattern in 2021: sampling produced higher-accuracy models that the confidence predictor *"was not able to consistently identify."* Two papers, five years apart, same diagnosis. Since every Level-1 design pipeline decides what to synthesize using exactly this machinery, ranking quality is the lever. Boltz-1's 48-layer trunk-cloned confidence model and Boltz-2's affinity head beating ipTM on MF-PCBA enrichment are both attacks on this problem; PXDesign's finding that Protenix and AF2-IG filters retain *different* true positives, with limited overlap, suggests ensembling across predictors is the cheap win.

**(b) Sampling compute substitutes for model quality, predictably.** Protenix-v1: 36.01% → 47.68% DockQ SR from 1 to 80 seeds. Protenix-v2 at 5 seeds beats Protenix-v1 at 1000. ESMFold2: 49% → 65% antibody-antigen from 1 to 1024 seeds, with the half-size MSA-free variant closing the entire gap to the full model by sampling alone. OpenDDE fits it as a cross-model scaling law. Any design pipeline that filters with a predictor has a compute dial on filter quality, and none of the design papers this project will review appear to report where on that curve they ran.

**(c) The antibody-antigen gap is where lineages actually separate.** On older benchmarks the models cluster within confidence intervals. On OpenDDE's fresh low-homology 2026ARK-AB set they spread 3–4× — 66.4% down to 16.7%. Given that antibodies and VHH formats are the dominant modality in current binder design (Chai-2, Protenix-v2, Latent-X2, ESMFold2's scFvs), this is the benchmark that should drive predictor choice in the design reports, and the older near-ties should be treated as saturation rather than parity.

### 9.4 The open/closed picture

The review's framing is open models, and the corpus supports it: AF2, RoseTTAFold, RFAA, Boltz-1/2, Protenix-v1/v2, OpenDDE, OpenFold3, ESM2/ESMFold1 and ESMC/ESMFold2 all ship weights. AF3 is the pivotal exception — its closure is the stated motivating gap behind Boltz-1, Protenix and OpenFold3 existing at all, and it is why Chai-1 could not benchmark against it for most tasks (licensing barred running it), why several AF3 columns in this doc are lifted from AF3's own released predictions rather than independently re-run, and why two of its published algorithms had to be corrected by a third party (§3).

Commercial systems can be mentioned but cannot be assessed. **IsoDDE** (Isomorphic Labs; no publication, no DOI) is the one that matters, and the only measurement available is second-hand: a reference level on OpenDDE's scaling curve, with OpenDDE stating it cannot attribute the remaining gap to architecture, data, scaling or inference because none of that is published. **Chai-3** is a web platform with no publication and no DOI. Treat both as landmarks on a map, not as entries in a comparison.

### 9.5 Handoff

Each section above is the parent chapter for one design report: §2 → BindCraft and AlphaProteo; §3 → BoltzGen and BoltzDesign1; §4 → PXDesign and Protenix-v2's design half (DOIs for all of these are in the §1.3 table); the RoseTTAFold lineage → RFdiffusion 1/2/3 and ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN, all now in `rfdiffusion_family_summary.md` and `proteinmpnn_ligandmpnn_summary.md`; §6 → Chai-2; §7 → the ESMC/ESMFold2 binder campaign. §5 has no descendant yet. Latent-X has no parent here.

The question to carry into each: **what coupling level does this pipeline achieve, and what did its parent predictor's limitations cost it?** The prediction that falls out of §1.2 is that Level-2 pipelines will cluster on predictors that are natively strong without MSAs — and that MSA-loyal lineages will reach for Level 0 or Level 1 instead, or else pay for Level 2 by running their predictor in single-sequence mode, off-distribution, with compensating machinery bolted on. BindCraft's four-stage annealing against ESMFold2's single-stage optimization is the first data point. Whether it generalizes is the thing to test across the design reports.

### 9.6 Corrections carried by this document

Recorded rather than silently fixed, so the same errors are not reintroduced downstream.

- **The two AF3 specification errors — half-restored.** An earlier draft claimed Boltz-1 and Protenix-v1 independently found and fixed the *same two* errors in AF3's published algorithms, and a first correction pass then withdrew the claim entirely. Direct reading of Boltz-1 shows the withdrawal went too far in one direction and not far enough in the other. **Boltz-1 documents both fixes**: the missing `DiffusionTransformer` residual connection (AF3 Algorithm 23), with a footnote calling such differences likely *"reporting mistakes"*; and the diffusion-loss weighting, where it uses the EDM form *"rather than"* AF3 Section 3.7.1 Eq. 6 — exactly as originally described. What remains unsupported is the **Protenix** half: the cataloged `protenix_v1` (Feb 2026) contains no table of AF3 corrections (§4). Boltz-1's documented independent convergence is with the Ligo Biosciences AF3 reimplementation, on the MSA-module reordering. So: two real errors in AF3's published spec, found by one team in this corpus, not two.
- **Protenix version confusion.** `protenix_v1` is the Feb 2026 ByteDance Seed paper. The 2024/2025 *"Protenix — advancing structure prediction through a comprehensive AlphaFold3 reproduction"* is a different preprint, cited by the 2026 paper as its own reference [19], describing the model generation shipped as Protenix v0.2.0/v0.5.0. Neither is peer-reviewed; "v1" is a model version, not a paper revision. Claims belonging to the earlier document are listed and withdrawn in §4.
- **Boltz-1 physical validity was inverted.** 57% is the *failure* rate; the pass rate is ~43%. Corrected throughout (§3).
- **PoseBusters versions.** AF3's detailed validity panel (Extended Data Fig. 4f) is PoseBusters **V2**; its headline ligand comparison is V1. The two are not interchangeable.
- **Smaller fixes:** AlQuraishi (not AlQuraissi); ESMC is Biohub-first with five co-first authors, with work performed at Biohub or EvolutionaryScale; ESMFold2-Fast halves the folding trunk (24 vs 48 layers) as well as dropping the MSA encoder; Chai-1/AF2.3's CASP15 numbers cover 69 targets against ESM3's 70; Protenix-v1 ships two checkpoints, so "one model, unlike AF3's two" no longer holds.
