##### **Abstract** 

13 Designing high-affinity de novo protein binders has become increasingly tractable, yet _in_ 14 _vitro_ prioritisation continues to depend on heuristics in the absence of systematic analysis. 15 Here, we present a large-scale meta-analysis of 3,766 experimentally tested binders across 15 16 structurally diverse targets. Using a unified, high-throughput pipeline that re-predicts each 17 binder–target complex with AF2 (initial guess and ColabFold), AF3 and Boltz-1, we extract 18 over 200 structural, energetic and confidence features per design. We show that interface19 focused metrics, most notably the AF3-derived interaction prediction Score from Aligned 20 Errors (ipSAE) outperform commonly used scores such as ipAE and ipTM, with a significant 21 1.4-fold increase in average precision compared to ipAE. We further show that combining these 22 metrics with orthogonal physicochemical interface descriptors, including Rosetta ∆G/∆SASA 23 and interface shape complementarity, improves predictive performance. While overall per24 formance varies by target, simple linear models trained on a small number of AF3-derived 25 features generalize well across datasets. We propose interpretable, target-agnostic filtering 26 strategies, such as combining AF3 ipSAE_min rankings with structural filters, to improve 27 precision in selecting binders for testing. Finally, we release the complete dataset establishing 28 a community resource to benchmark and accelerate de novo binder discovery.

## **Introduction** 

- 30 Recent advances in de novo protein binder design have opened new possibilities for computational 31 protein engineering. Methods such as RFdiffusion [1], BindCraft [2], and AlphaProteo [3] now en32 able the generation of high-affinity protein binders directly from target structures, without relying 33 on natural templates. These methods have already been applied across a wide spectrum of use 34 cases [4–13], highlighting their potential in therapeutics, diagnostics, and basic research. 

- 35 The synthesis and screening of large libraries of protein binders remain costly and labour intensive. 36 Consequently, the _in silico_ filtering of candidate designs has become a central challenge. Filtering 37 de novo designed proteins is especially challenging, as it usually requires selection among a highly 38 similar pool of structures. 

- 39 A major advance in de novo binder design was the discovery that deep learning–based protein 40 structure prediction models especially AlphaFold2 (AF2) [14] can effectively prioritize candidates 41 prior to experimental validation, increasing the experimental success rate. Confidence metrics de42 rived from AF2 predictions, such as per-residue confidence (pLDDT), interaction predicted aligned 43 error (ipAE), and interface predicted TM-score (ipTM), have been shown to be predictive of _in_ 44 _vitro_ binding, outperforming traditional physics-based scores such as Rosetta energies [15].

- 45 Despite this progress, design success remains very inconsistent, and there are no standard criteria 46 to prioritise binders for experimental testing. Since the adoption of AF2-based filtering, several 47 newer and more accurate protein structure prediction models have emerged, including AlphaFold3 48 (AF3) [16], Boltz-1 [17], Boltz-2 [18] and Chai-1 [19]. However, their performance in prediction of 49 _in vitro_ binding has not been systematically evaluated. In parallel, a growing number of alternative 50 metrics and filters have been proposed to improve scoring of protein–protein interactions [10,20–26], 51 yet their generalisability across large, diverse datasets remains unclear. This is corroborated by 52 most de novo campaigns producing only a small number of validated binders and typically focusing 53 on related targets and thus limiting the ability to benchmark scoring methods at scale. 

- 54 To tackle these limitations, we curated a dataset of over 3,700 experimentally tested de novo binders 55 targeting 15 structurally and functionally diverse proteins. To score these designs efficiently at 56 scale, we developed a streamlined pipeline that re-predicts each binder–target complex using four 57 structure prediction tools: the initial guess and ColabFold implementations of AF2 [15, 27], as 58 well as AF3 and Boltz-1. We benchmarked the impact of multiple sequence alignment (MSA) 59 generation, number of recycles, and number of models on both runtime and prediction accuracy, 60 identifying configurations that substantially improve throughput with minimal loss in model con61 fidence. Together, we extracted over 200 structural, energetic, confidence, and sequence-based fea62 tures per design.This large-scale meta-analysis enabled us to systematically evaluate which metrics 63 best predict experimental success across targets, design protocols, and prediction methods.

### **Overview of data** 

- 66 To assemble a dataset spanning diverse target classes and binder designs, we curated experimentally 67 tested de novo binders from multiple published sources (Table 1). In total, we collected data from 68 binders covering 15 distinct targets, including receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), pathogen-derived 

- 69 antigens, immune modulators, and a range of additional targets such as a snake venom short-chain 70 _α_ -neurotoxin (sntx), a proto-oncogene, and two peptide MHC (pMHC) complexes. ### **Features that predict binding** 

- 111 We first aimed to identify which features from our scoring pipeline best discriminated between 112 experimentally verified binders and non-binders across all data. The distributions of some features 113 were significantly different between binders and non-binders, while other features had very similar 114 distributions (Supplementary Fig. 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). We also observed that the template-based 115 AF2 initial guess structures were distinct from those generated by ColabFold, Boltz-1 and AF3, 116 which tended to produce more similar structures among themselves. This distinction was evident 117 both in terms of RMSD between predicted structures and in structural metrics such as interface 118 size, ∆SASA and interface nonpolar residue count (Supplementary Fig. 7, 8). 

- 119 We next quantified the ability of each feature to separate binders from non-binders using average 120 precision (AP), which avoids the need to set arbitrary thresholds and is preferable to the area under 121 the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) given the strong class imbalance favouring 122 non-binders [28]. Using AP, we identified the best individual features and in addition examined 123 the effects of feature interactions, by trying all pairwise products within each structure prediction 124 models feature set (Fig. **3** A). For AF3, ColabFold and Boltz-1 the best individual features were 125 ipSAE_min, ipSAE_max and LIS, all of which are scores based on the pAE matrix and corrected 126 to only capture the high confidence binding interface. ipSAE is calculated similarly to ipTM, 

- 127 but only considers interchain residue pairs below a pAE cutoff (<10 by default). Similarly, LIS

### **Feature combination by greedy feature selection** 

- 207 After analysing which individual and interaction features best discriminated _in vitro_ binders from 208 non-binders, we aimed to identify whether using multiple features in a linear combination allows for 209 improved selection of true binders. This was based on a twofold hypothesis: (i) agreement among 

- 210 confidence metrics from different structure prediction tools may carry meaningful information, 211 and (ii) structural and physicochemical metrics may provide complementary, orthogonal insights 

- 212 to those confidence metrics. To this end, we established a greedy feature selection procedure using 

8

- 213 logistic regression in a Leave-One-Group-Out cross-validation approach, with each of the 15 targets 214 as the holdout group. During greedy feature selection, features were selected in an inner cross215 validation loop to maximize the median AP across the inner holdout targets, with early stopping 216 of feature addition if AP did not increase more than 0.005 to prevent overfitting. The selected 

- 217 feature sets and corresponding logistic regression models were then evaluated on the outer holdout 218 targets. We applied this procedure separately to individual features and to combined individual 

- 219 + interaction feature sets for each structure prediction tool. Interaction features were included 220 to capture non-linear relationships that single-term models cannot represent. We also applied 221 the procedure to a combined feature pool containing features from all structure prediction tools 222 (Fig. **4** A). 

- 223 This analysis allowed us to assess whether features from different structure prediction models were 224 equally informative, or whether combining features across models provided complementary value. 225 The approach produced (i) an interpretable model in which feature importance can be inferred 226 directly from the regression coefficients, and (ii) a feature set that only expands when features 227 generalize well across different targets. To confirm that these results were not biased by redundancy 228 in the dataset, we evaluated sequence-based similarity for both targets and binders. Apart from 229 the expected similarity between the two pMHC targets and an observed similarity within the 230 pMHC NY1 binders, both target and binder datasets exhibited a high degree of sequence diversity 231 (Supplementary Fig. 1A,B). 

- 232 While none of the models outperformed the baseline LIS × input_interface_shape_ 233 complementarity (median AP = 0.58, IQR: 0.33–0.66) the model using individual AF3-derived 234 features obtained a very comparable median AP (0.57) with reduced variance (IQR: 0.40–0.66), 235 and it outperformed the best individual feature baseline AF3 ipSAE_min (Fig. **4** A). These two 236 findings show that adding information from structural features can improve the prediction of the 237 best confidence metrics, but the additional predictive power is easily exhausted. On average, only 238 2 to 5 features were added per model in the inner loop before improvement stagnated (Supple239 mentary Fig. 13B), and when comparing performance in the outer loop to the best individual AF3 240 feature model, an increase in AP was observed only for a subset of targets (Fig. **4** B). Combining all 241 features from every structure prediction model did not improve median AP, even though it was the 242 top performer on a handful of targets (Supplementary Fig. 13A). These results suggest that incor243 porating features from other models seems to only introduce noise rather than add complementary 244 information across targets. Similarly, adding interaction features did not lead to performance im245 provements, when predicting on unseen targets. Adding more features likely increases the models 246 susceptibility to overfitting. Across all models less features got selected in the inner loop for mod247 els using interaction features (Supplementary Fig. 13B), indicating that most interaction terms do 248 not generalize well across targets and likely provide redundant information. Consistent with these 249 findings, switching to a more complex model such as XGBoost, which can capture nonlinear rela250 tionships, did not yield any performance improvement (Supplementary Fig. 14B). This reinforces 

- 251 the conclusion that only a small set of individual features generalize well across targets, and that 252 the overall predictive signal is better captured by a simple linear model using these few informative 253 features. 

- 254 To investigate which features from the model using all AF3-derived individual features were most 255 relevant and potentially contributed predictive power, we examined both the frequency with which 256 features were selected in the inner loop and their average logistic regression coefficients (Fig. **4** C). 257 Strikingly, AF3 ipSAE_min was selected in all cases, highlighting once again the robustness and 258 predictive strength of this single feature. Beyond that, both the selection frequency and average 259 coefficient values dropped sharply, suggesting that no other individual feature consistently improve 260 prediction across targets. Notably, two features based on structural comparisons (C _α_ RMSD) 

- 261 between the input and AF3 structure were frequently selected: one comparing the binder structures 262 (after aligning the binder-target complex on the target) and one comparing the full complexes. 263 Additionally, input_interface_shape_complementarity and interface_∆G/∆SASA were among 264 the selected features supporting our earlier findings that these physicochemical interface descriptor 265 can add complementary information to confidence based features.

- 298 clear strategies for prioritising _in silico_ designs for experimental testing are still lacking. In this 299 study, we present a large-scale benchmark of structural, energetic, confidence, and sequence-based 300 features for predicting binding success in de novo protein-binder campaigns. To this end we curated 

- 301 a dataset of 3,766 binder-target designs from 15 campaigns and re-predicted every complex using 

- 302 four structure prediction tools: the initial guess and ColabFold implementations of AlphaFold2 303 (AF2), AlphaFold3 (AF3) as well as its open-source implementation Boltz-1 [15–17, 27]. For this 

- 304 we applied a streamlined prediction setup that reduced runtime by 87% while preserving interface305 confidence accuracy. This enabled large-scale benchmarking and provides a practical, generalisable 306 framework for evaluating confidence and structure-based metrics in protein binder design. Crit307 ically, it allowed us to identify metrics and combinations of metrics that correlate with binding 308 success and improve the success rate over commonly used scores for binder selection. 

- 309 AF3-derived metrics consistently show the highest predictive performance, even against the most 310 comparable model, Boltz-1. This observation aligns with previous findings that AF3 outperforms 311 other models in protein–protein interaction prediction tasks [17, 18]. However, we note that the 312 recently described Boltz-2 [18] and other models such as Chai-1 [19] may offer improved accuracy 313 and warrant further evaluation. 

- 314 We found that AF3 ipSAE_min was the best-performing predictor across all targets. The ipSAE 315 metric [22] is a high-confidence-interface focused scoring scheme, and we specifically found that 316 aggregating asymmetric predictions between chains using the minimum, rather than the average or 317 maximum, yielded the most discriminative results. AF3 ipSAE_min provides a 1.4-fold increase in 318 average precision (AP) compared to the AF2 initial guess pAE interaction (ipAE) score, which is 319 commonly used to filter _in silico_ designs of the state-of-the-art de novo design tool RFdiffusion [1]. 

- 320 This improvement may stem from the fact that ipSAE focuses on high-confidence interfacial residue 

- 321 pairs and scales by interface size, leading to more consistent and target-agnostic performance com322 pared to ipAE [22]. We note that some data in this study had already been pre-selected using 323 AF2 initial guess ipAE, potentially deflating the apparent predictive power of this metric. How324 ever, the ipAE score for Boltz-1, ColabFold and AF3 remained also suboptimal. The ipTM score, 325 another commonly used scoring metric (e.g., in BindCraft [2]) had lower AP across targets com326 pared to AF3 ipSAE_min, however it still performed well, especially at low recall thresholds. 327 Our analysis further revealed that combining features through pairwise products can enhance pre328 dictive performance, by capturing interaction effects. Notably, the best performing interaction 329 features combined interface focused confidence metric such as ipSAE or LIS [23] and a physico330 chemical interface descriptors such as the Rosetta based shape_complementary, ∆G/∆SASA or 331 ∆G. Consistent with this observation, a recent study focused on nanobody binding prediction also 332 highlighted the importance of energy based metrics as predictive features [29]. Together, these 333 findings underscore the potential of integrating orthogonal feature types that are physically based, 334 in addition to confidence scores to improve experimental success rates in de novo binder design 335 campaigns. 

- 336 Despite the improved predictive performance of individual and interaction metrics, we observed 337 substantial variability across different targets. For AF3 ipSAE_min the precision ranged from 0.1 338 to 1.0 across targets at the maximum F1 threshold. We observed a higher AP of AF3_ipSAE_min 

- 339 for targets with high energy density in the binding interface as captured by higher H-bond percent340 age or more negative ∆G/∆SASA ratio. Although the observed correlations with predictability 341 were relatively weak, they may offer insights into the underlying factors that make some targets 342 inherently more difficult to predict and design successful binders for. Notably, AF3 ipSAE_min 343 AP did not correlate with interface hydrophobicity (∆SAP), despite its known association with 344 binder success [30]. This correlation remained for AF2 ipAE, suggesting that AF3 ipSAE_min 345 captures different aspects of interface quality and does not rely on hydrophobic burial. 

- 346 We next evaluated whether combining multiple features in a linear fashion, rather than constructing 347 interaction terms, could improve predictive performance across targets. We approached this using 348 greedy feature selection with a logistic regression model and nested cross-validation, where each 349 target was held out once and performance was evaluated using the median AP across holdout 350 groups. This setup was designed to obtain an interpretable model that only selects features if they 351 generalize well across targets. Notably, one model outperformed the best individual feature, but 352 not the single best interaction feature in terms of AP across all targets, and most models selected 353 only a small number of features. This suggests that only a few features consistently perform 354 well across diverse targets and highlights the difficulty of building generalizable predictive models 355 for scoring binders against novel targets. However, for the best-performing model using AF3-

- 375 As a first step, our analysis demonstrates how to efficiently predict de novo binders and identifies 376 which structure-prediction models perform best. Along with our actionable recommendations for 377 candidate selection, the accompanying open-source dataset and pipeline provide a robust bench378 mark for the community. Together, these resources enable the systematic evaluation of structure379 based binder scoring methods and will help accelerate the rapidly growing field of de novo protein 380 design.

426 **Confidence scores** 

- 427 From each structure predictor the relevant confidence scores (from highest ranked model as well as 428 average and standard deviation across sub models) were collected and the PDB file from the highest 429 ranked model was extracted. - 432 with <12 pAE) [23], pDockQ [37] and pDockQ2 [24] scores were calculated as described in [22]. 

- 433 The ipSAE is calculated in a similar manner as ipTM, but instead of the interchain comparison 434 employed in ipTM, it only included residue pairs below a certain pAE cutoff, and dynamically 435 adjusts the _d_ 0 parameter according to the number of residues within the cutoff. The value of _d_ 0 436 increases with the square root of the number of interfacing residues. In turn, this means that a 437 longer interface (with similar pAE values) results in a higher ipSAE score, which down-weights 438 small but confident interaction surfaces, and reflects that very short interfaces are unlikely to result 439 in binding. 



- 440 In addition to the ipSAE score versions previously reported (using different d0 corrections) [22], 441 we also introduced some further modifications to ipSAE. Since the ipSAE score is asymmetrical, 442 we stored both the max and min ipSAE score of any binder _→_ target chain or target _→_ binder 443 chain value (denoted ipSAE_min and ipSAE_max). If the target had several subchains we took 444 the average of the min and max values across both directions of binder _→_ target comparisons, but 445 only if there are interacting residues between the binder chain and a given target subchain. In the 446 standard implementation (ipSAE_max), the asymmetric score is summarized by the max value, 447 but we reasoned that the ’weakest link’ might be most informative for binding status. Furthermore, 448 we also explored replacing max operator across chain A in the calculation of ipSAE, with an average 449 or min, with the intuition of increasing the richness or stringency of the score respectively. We 450 reported these scores as ipSAE_avg and ipSAE_min_in_calculation. We also calculated the 451 ipAE from the pAE matrixes of Boltz1, ColabFold and AF3. This was calculated as an average 452 of interchain pAE value from the binder chain to all other chains, following the implementation 453 in [15].

### 465 **Logistic regression classifier** 

- 466 All numeric features were normalized using z-score standardization with statistics from the current 467 training split; class imbalance ( _∼_ 12%binders) was handled with the inbuilt `class_weight="balanced"` 468 which up-weighs the underrepresented true binders in the fit. 

- 469 In the greedy forward-selection procedure, we left one target out at a time and used a Leave-One470 Group-Out cross-validation over the remaining targets to choose features. Starting from an empty 471 set, at each iteration we evaluated every candidate feature by training an l1-penalized logistic 472 regression (with solver="liblinear") on the inner folds and measuring median average precision 473 (AP). The feature whose inclusion yielded the largest AP gain was added, stopping once no re474 maining candidate improved AP by at least 0.005. After each feature was added, the model was 475 also evaluated on the held-out target to track how performance based on AP evolved on the outer 476 loop. Once selection finished, the final model was retrained on the inner data with C parameter 477 optimization and evaluated on the outer set to report the final AP. 

- 478 This greedy routine was run separately for each structure prediction model (AF2 initial guess, 479 AF3, Boltz-1, and ColabFold), using their model-specific features together with the input structure 480 features and general descriptors (binder/target length, spatialPPI, and CamSol score). For each 481 case, models were trained either with all individual features or with all individual features plus the 482 top 50 interaction terms (ranked by AP across targets) to reduce model complexity. In addition, two 483 combined runs were performed, using either all individual features across models or all individual 484 + interaction features across structure prediction models.
